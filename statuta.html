About this edition

What follows is the text of the Statuta Fundamentalia itself, word for word, with its own numbering. Nothing has been reworded. Provisions that concern the Collegium’s inward order among its officers are not reproduced, and each place is marked in the text. The Articles on ranks, awards, fees, review and appeal, and disclosure are given in full.

Where this edition and the full Statuta Fundamentalia differ, the full Statuta govern (Section 11.4(9)).

Preamble & Archiepiscopal Decree of Erection (Decretum Erectionis)

In the Name of the Holy and Undivided Trinity: God the Father, the Primordial Power and Source of All; God the Son, the Incarnate Love and Word of the Father; and God the Holy Spirit, the Sovereign Wisdom and Life-Giver; the One True God, Active, Manifest, and All-Sustaining, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.
To all Prelates, Clergy, Scholars, Mystics, and Practitioners of the Sacred Sciences to whom these Letters Patent shall come, Greeting in the Eternal Spirit of Wisdom and Love:
WHEREAS, in the historic canonical and apostolic tradition of the One, Holy, Catholic, and Apostolic Church, an Archbishop and autonomous Prelate holds the inherent canonical oversight and pastoral authority (jus patronatus) as chief didaskalos (teacher of the faith), shepherd, and mystagogue: to establish centres of theological study, to charter holy learned societies (collegia academica), and to patronise scriptoria for the safeguarding, illumination, and defence of sacred tradition;
AND WHEREAS, the luminous spiritual synthesis, sacramental science, and convergent apostolic heritage championed by our revered spiritual predecessors: His Sacred Beatitude Patriarch +++Herman Adrian Spruit (1911–1994), Matriarch +++Meri Spruit, His Eminence Primate Emeritus Archbishop +++Frank R. Bugge, and Her Grace Archbishop ++Leonie Newman (1951–2024), constitute a vital treasure of Christian mysticism, metaphysical sciences, and sacramental justice within the Independent Sacramental Movement (ISM);
NOW THEREFORE, We, The Most Reverend Mar Elisha Thoma, by Divine Providence and Apostolic Succession an Archbishop and autonomous Prelate, who by the laying on of hands in the venerable lines of the Australian Church of Antioch (Spruit–Bugge), as the Register of Succession of that Church records, have received the Syro-Antiochene (Alvares–Vilatte; Boutros–Ferrette), Syro-Chaldean (Thondanatta–Herford), Old Catholic (Varlet–Mathew) and Anglican (Moore–White) successions, among others, acting in the fullness of Our episcopal prerogative (ratione personae et officii), do hereby formally erect, constitute, and charter The Herman Adrian Spruit Research Collegium (HASRC) as an autonomous, self-governing Ecclesiastical Learned Society, Research Centre, and Digital Scriptorium, to be governed in perpetuity in accordance with the solemn Articles and Statutes set forth hereunder and the Ordinances made under them.
Article I

Name, Canonical Personality & Extraterritorial Status

Sections 1.1 to 1.3 are entrenched and immutable, save as Section 11.2 provides. Section 1.4 is not entrenched and may be amended by Decree of the Provost under Section 11.1.
Terms written with capital initials, such as Chancery, Register, Collegian, Custodian, Decree of the Provost and Ordinances, are defined in Section 11.4.

§ 1.1Official Style and Title

1. Style. The institution is formally styled The Herman Adrian Spruit Research Collegium, abbreviated in all charters, seals, and post-nominal designations as HASRC (hereinafter referred to as "the Collegium").
2. Heritage Style. In heritage and future documents as well as archival imprints, the style Herman Adrian Spruit Research Centre is recognised as synonymous with and subsumed into the Collegium.

§ 1.2Canonical Personality (Persona Juridica Canonica)

1. Nature. The Collegium is erected as an autonomous ecclesiastical collegiate society (Collegium Eruditorum Ecclesiasticum) possessing distinct canonical personality under the archiepiscopal protection and personal jurisdiction of its Founder and Provost.
2. Functional Character. It operates not as a territorial parish or geographic diocese, but as a functional, scholarly, and contemplative sodality of collegians worldwide.
3. Autocephaly. In its own governance the Collegium is autocephalous, that is, self-headed under its Provost, and is subject to no external synod.

§ 1.3Extraterritorial Digital Seat

1. Digital Seat. The primary operational seat, registry, and scriptorium of the Collegium exist in the global digital commons and its affiliated archival repositories.
2. Extraterritorial Character. As an extraterritorial ecclesiastical learned society and digital scriptorium, the Collegium conducts no public commercial trade and maintains complete separation from secular municipal jurisdictions.
3. Civil Status. As at the date of these Statutes the Collegium is unincorporated.
4. Civil Registration. Any civil registration by the Collegium or by a Civil Steward under Section 10.6 for a limited purpose (Section 10.5(2)) does not alter its canonical personality or sovereignty.
5. Not-for-Profit Purposes. Any such registration is for not-for-profit purposes only.
6. Article X and Trust. The Collegium remains bound by Article X, and the registered body holds its funds and property for the Collegium under Section 10.4 and acts at the direction of the Provost.

§ 1.4Chartered Collegiate Chapters & Oratories (De Capitulis et Oratoriis)

The Collegium grows as a living fellowship. Wherever Collegians gather to study, pray and serve, the Collegium rejoices to see its work take root.
1. Warrant of Constitution. Three (3) or more Collegians in good standing may petition the Provost for a Warrant of Constitution, which the Provost shall grant gladly where the Foundational Statutes are honoured. Chapters and Oratories stand as equals, in direct communion with the central Chancery (the Provost’s administrative office, Section 11.4(1)), without intermediate regional or national hierarchy.
Collegiate Chapter (Capitulum Academicum): a scholarly fellowship of the mind, for colloquia, study of the Antiochian heritage (Spruitian Charism), meditation, and reunions, led by a Chapter Warden (lay or ordained, of AHASRC, LHASRC or FHASRC standing).
Oratory (Oratorium): a fellowship of the heart, for prayer, meditation, healing prayer and the Collegium’s Order of Prayer, and, on appointed Eucharistic Days, for the Holy Eucharist celebrated by an invited priest.
Collegians who chiefly wish to study petition for a Chapter. Those who chiefly wish to pray petition for an Oratory. Those who wish both may be chartered together under one Warrant as a Combined Foundation.
2. Self-Governance in Communion. Each Chapter and Oratory governs its own local life: its programme, schedule, local officers, hospitality and study themes. It does so under its own Constitution, adopted from the Model Constitution issued by the Chancery. No local Constitution may contradict the Foundational Statutes or the Spruitian Charism. Wardens and Chaplains, the support the Chancery gives to branches, the Annual Return, dormancy and lapse, and the honour of Senior Warden and Senior Chaplain are set out in Ordinance 1.
3. Liturgical & Ceremonial Right. Members may assemble in the name of the Collegium in official academic regalia for sacred liturgy, contemplative practice and academic gatherings.
4. Local Financial Stewardship. The Collegium levies no enrolment, examination, assessment or rank fee (Section 10.5). Chapters and Oratories freely keep local altar offerings, retreat hospitality contributions and study-circle dues to sustain their gatherings. None of these may be required for, or linked to, admission, assessment, rank or diploma, and no rank-related fee may be charged in the Collegium’s name.
5. Optional Civil Registration & Liability Firewall. A branch may choose to incorporate, but incorporation is optional. The Collegium supports incorporation for good governance, and a branch’s existence, standing and Warrant remain under these Statutes whether or not it incorporates. A branch that registers a civil entity or leases premises does so at its own legal, tax and financial responsibility. No branch, Warden or Chaplain may bind the Provost or the Collegium to any debt, contract, lease or liability.
6. Reserved Matters. Ranks (AHASRC, LHASRC, FHASRC) and diplomas are conferred only by the central Chancery under the Great Seal. Use of the Collegium’s name, insignia and marks is by licence of the Warrant.
7. Fraternal Counsel & Revocation. In case of serious departure from collegiate order, the Provost first offers fraternal counsel, then written notice and a fair chance to respond. The Provost may suspend a Warrant immediately where urgent harm requires it. Upon suspension or revocation, the branch ceases using the Collegium’s name, insignia and marks.
8. Order of Prayer and Approved Rites. The Order of Prayer and the rites of the branches are governed as follows:
(a) The Provost promulgates an Order of Prayer for Oratories, which anyone may use freely, but which only a chartered branch may present as the Collegium’s own.
(b) An Oratory uses only the ACOA Rite, the Authorised Eucharistic Liturgy of the Australian Church of Antioch, so far as the ACOA permits its use by the Collegium, whether by Concordat under Section 3.2 or by written consent recorded in the Register of Approved Rites, and such other rites as the Provost approves in writing and records in that Register.
(c) The Collegium does not ordain, license or rule on the validity of any celebrant’s orders.
(d) No rite or practice that harms, demeans or coerces anyone, or is contrary to the love, dignity and good of humanity, may be used by any branch.
9. Safeguarding and Care. Every Chapter and Oratory adopts the safeguarding provisions of the Model Constitution, and the following apply:
(a) No one is to be left alone in ministry with a child or an adult at risk, and concerns are reported to the Chancery without delay.
(b) Nothing in a branch’s life may discourage a person from seeking medical, psychological or other professional help.
(c) A concern about any person, including an officer of the Collegium, may also be reported to the civil authorities and to that person’s own church, and nothing in these Statutes prevents it.
(d) Nothing in these Statutes overrides any duty to report that the law of the place where a branch meets imposes.
(e) Prayer, meditation and healing prayer in a branch are spiritual practices and are not offered as medical, psychological or other regulated treatment.
Article II

Spiritual Charism, Legacy & Guiding Principles

The Collegium is anchored in the living spiritual charism of the Antiochian tradition as articulated and transmitted by:
1. 2. His Eminence Primate Emeritus Archbishop +++Frank R. Patriarch Spruit handed over and entrusted to him the guardianship of nineteen apostolic successions, being the seventeen lines of Spruit together with the two lines received from Archmage Ryan and Archdruid Cluthin. 3. His Eminence Primate, Archbishop +++Glenn W. 4. Archbishop ++Leonie Newman (1951–2024): Consecrated by Archbishop Bugge in 2014; Late Primate of The Temple of Ancient Wisdom and Archbishop within the ACOA, remembered for her dedicated preservation of primordial esoteric studies and spiritual healing rites.
The Collegium confesses the Holy Trinity as the Church of the Nicene Creed confesses it, and welcomes with respect those who read the names of the Persons by other lights.

§ 2.2Codification of the Spruitian Charism

The Collegium’s own scholarship, publications and curricula shall remain faithful to the five pillars of the Spruitian Charism, which the Collegium holds as its inheritance. The pillars do not bind the inquiry or the conclusions of any candidate or Collegian, which remain free under Section 5.7(3). The pillars are teachings of the Collegium’s tradition, in which spirituality is studied as a science in the older sense of scientia, ordered knowledge, which the Collegium’s motto joins to wisdom, though not as a laboratory science. They are not offered as a replacement for medical or psychological care (Section 9.1). The pillars are:
1. Metaphysical-Sacramental Synthesis: The harmonious fusion of traditional Catholic sacramental realism (understanding the seven historic sacraments as objective conduits of divine grace and subtle energy, informed by the Theosophical and Liberal Catholic literature on the science of the sacraments) with the experiential principles of New Thought, Metaphysical Science, spiritual mind healing, and creative consciousness.
2. Christ-Centred Universalism & Meta-Catholicity: Grounding theological inquiry in the living, personal, and cosmic Christ, wherein unconditional Love is revered as the supreme spiritual reality, transcending rigid dogmatism and sectarian exclusivity.
3. Sacramental Justice & Radical Inclusion: Upholding the principle of the Open Altar, welcoming all seekers to the Eucharist with dignity regardless of marital status, gender, orientation, or background, and affirming universal equality in Holy Orders, safeguarding the unbroken female and male apostolic lineages.
4. Apostolic Convergence: Honouring the gathering and preservation of historic streams of Apostolic Succession not as instruments of bureaucratic legalism, but as a mystical sacrament of Christian unity and spiritual empowerment.
5. Spiritual Engineering & Altar Energetics: Bridging theoretical scholarship with practical spiritual progression through the development of contemplative protocols, liturgical metaphysics, clairvoyant altar dynamics, and transformative inner technologies.

§ 2.3The Six Core Values (WRITES)

The Collegium conducts its work in strict accordance with six foundational values, encapsulated by the acronym WRITES:
W — Wisdom & Mysticism: Embracing perennial mystical wisdom and ancient esoteric traditions as essential lenses for understanding metaphysical reality.
R — Respectful Inquiry: Fostering intellectual empathy, academic rigour, and non-dogmatic exploration across comparative spiritual traditions.
I — Inclusive Diversity: Welcoming scholars, clergy, practitioners, and seekers from all backgrounds into a collaborative collegiate fellowship.
T — Transformative Knowledge: Advancing scholarship that catalyses personal illumination, ethical leadership, and conscious evolution.
E — Excellence in Research & Education: Upholding rigorous bibliographic, analytical, and ethical standards in all publications and study tracks.
S — Spiritual Exploration: Cultivating the inner spiritual essence of both scholar and practitioner as the true foundation of sacred insight.

§ 2.4Four Institutional Best Practices

1. Spiritual & Charism Fidelity: Grounding the Collegium’s own research and training in the authentic inner-Christian and metaphysical charism of our spiritual predecessors.
2. Open & Ethical Methodology: Maintaining transparency of sources, intellectual honesty, and absolute respect for the free will and well-being of all participants.
3. Practical & Contextual Responsibility: Ensuring that metaphysical rites, meditations, and energetic techniques are taught within a sound, responsible ethical framework.
4. Inclusive & Respectful Dialogue: Engaging constructively with the wider Independent Sacramental Movement across theological differences, wider Christian communities, religious and secular academia, and interfaith communities.

§ 2.5The Feast of Holy Sophia

1. The Feast. The Feast of Holy Sophia is a feast of the Collegium. It is kept on 30 September, which is 17 September in the Julian reckoning, the day on which the Churches that keep the Julian calendar commemorate the holy martyrs Faith, Hope and Love and their mother Sophia.
2. The Old Reckoning. The Collegium keeps the old reckoning because it is the calendar of the Roman world in which the Church of Antioch arose. This is no slight to those who keep the Revised Julian calendar, and any Collegian may keep the feast on 17 September with equal honour.
3. Observance. Collegians are encouraged to assemble where more than two are present. A Collegian who is alone keeps the feast by meditation, the lighting of a candle, and the short prayer of the Collegium as the Provost may promulgate in the Order of Prayer.
4. Never a Condition. Keeping the feast is encouraged and is never a condition of rank or good standing.
5. Contemplative Reading. In the Collegium’s contemplative tradition the day is read in this way. The number 30 is three and a circle: the three daughters, Faith, Hope and Love, held within the unending circle of their mother Sophia, Wisdom, for which the zero (śūnya, the fullness that Indian numerals gave the world) may stand. The Hebrew letter Lamed has the value 30. It is the letter of learning and teaching, and its stem rises above all the others. In the Kabbalah, Wisdom (Chokhmah) is the source from which understanding and the lower emanations flow. The old date, 17, reduces to 8, the eighth day of new creation, which turned on its side is the sign of infinity. The feast falls in the weeks after the autumn equinox in the north and the spring equinox in the south, when light and dark stand in balance. It comes the day after Michaelmas, about a month before Samhain, the turning of the Celtic year, and in the weeks before Advent, when the Church prepares for the coming of the Light. These readings are offered for reflection. They bind no Collegian and are no claim of fact.
Article III

Canonical Affiliation, Concordat Amity & Autonomy

§ 3.1Sacramental Fellowship with the Australian Church of Antioch (ACOA)

1. Communion with the ACOA. The Collegium operates in solemn canonical amity, spiritual pledge, and sacramental fellowship with its mother communion, The Australian Church of Antioch (ACOA).
2. Primacy. The Collegium honours the Primacy of the ACOA, whose Primate is first among equals (primus inter pares) among its bishops, and honours the ACOA as the principal historical custodian of the Spruit-Bugge succession.
3. Concordat Amity. The Collegium offers Concordat Amity and Mutual Patronage to the ACOA, St. Michael’s Divinity School and every sister jurisdiction, as brothers and sisters in Christ, each honouring the other in charity and goodwill. Any Concordat itself is made under Section 3.2.

§ 3.2Concordats and Articulation Agreements

1. Power to Make Agreements: The Provost may, by written instrument under the Great Seal, make a Concordat with any church, order, jurisdiction or society, and an articulation agreement with any seminary, school or institution of learning, on whatever terms the Provost judges good for the Collegium and in keeping with these Statutes and Section 3.3.
2. Effect on Signature: An agreement takes effect when the other party and the Provost, under the Great Seal, have both signed it. The Chancery then enters it in the Register of Concordats and Articulations under item 3. Until it is signed, no rank, diploma, publication or statement of the Collegium states or implies that any other body recognises, accepts or credits any rank of the Collegium.
3. The Register of Concordats and Articulations: The Chancery keeps the Register of Concordats and Articulations, as part of the Register defined in Section 11.4(2), and publishes it on the Collegium’s website. For each agreement it records the date, the parties, the scope, the standing recorded for each rank and route, and any notice of variation or ending.
4. Benefit by Rank: An agreement in force applies to every holder of the rank concerned who is in good standing when the holder petitions, whenever the rank was conferred, unless the agreement says otherwise. A later variation or ending does not reduce standing already granted by the other body.
5. A Right to Petition, Not a Promise: Where an agreement so provides, a holder may petition the other body for advanced standing, recognition or candidacy. Admission, credit and any award are decided by the other body alone, under its own regulations. The Collegium promises no one acceptance, and charges no fee for confirming a rank to the other body.
6. No Payment Between Bodies: No fee, commission or referral payment passes between the Collegium and any other body by reason of an agreement. Any tuition, examination or graduation fee of the other body is paid by the holder directly to that body.
7. The Ranks Stand Alone: Every rank, diploma, post-nominal and right of regalia of the Collegium exists in full on conferral under the Great Seal. None depends on any agreement under this Section, and none is reduced if an agreement is delayed, varied or ended (Section 5.6).
8. Making and Ending: An agreement is made, varied or ended only by the Provost, or a successor holding the office in full, under the Great Seal, and by no other person except as the Provost consents in writing for a particular matter under these Statutes.
9. Honest Description: The Collegium describes every partner truthfully and only in the terms of the agreement, and asks each partner to do the same. A society formed outside the succession of either party is not that party.

§ 3.3Principle of Operational Sovereignty

Entrenched and immutable, save as Section 11.2 provides.
1. Autonomy: The Collegium is, and shall ever remain, an autonomous and self-governing canonical juridic person (Persona Juridica Canonica, Section 1.2), governed in peace by these Statutes under its Provost.
2. Internal Order: The ordering of its internal affairs rests with the Collegium alone, as the ordering of each sister body’s affairs rests with that body.
3. No Dependence: The Collegium stands and acts by these Statutes and its Provost. Its existence, its ranks and its work do not wait upon, and are not conditional on, the assent or agreement of any other body. Any agreement made under Section 3.2 adds to its life without making it dependent.
4. No Outside Authority: No other synod, consistory, church or prelate holds visitorial authority over the Collegium, or any power to depose the Provost, alter these Statutes, take its archives or property, or dissolve it. Each sister body is honoured as equally free in its own house.
5. The Provost’s Own Capacity: The Provost acts for the Collegium in its own right, and not as the representative of any other body. Whatever office the Provost holds in any other church or body, and whatever duty the Provost owes there, is personal to the Provost and does not bind the Collegium or give that body any authority over it.
6. Stewardship: Its Statutes, its offices, and its digital and archival heritage are held and stewarded by the Collegium itself, so that it may serve its sister bodies freely and faithfully.
7. Succession Qualification: The qualification of the Provost under Section 4.5 is the Collegium’s own rule, kept in honour of its mother communion, and gives no other body authority over the Collegium.

§ 3.4The Chancellor & Pro-Chancellors (De Cancellario)

1. Chancellor and Director. The Provost bears the styles Chancellor and Director under Section 4.1. As Chancellor the Provost signs diplomas and presides at convocations. As Director the Provost acts for the Collegium in secular dealings. The style Director creates no corporate directorship unless the Provost accepts one in writing.
4. Ceremonial Functions. At the Provost’s invitation, a Pro-Chancellor may countersign diplomas, preside over or bless convocations and graduations, represent the Collegium’s goodwill to sister bodies, and give counsel when asked.
5. An Office of Honour. The office of Pro-Chancellor is an office of honour, fellowship and goodwill, held in friendship with the Collegium. It carries no administrative burden and no liability. In particular:
(a) So that a Pro-Chancellor is spared the cares of governance, and so that the independence of the Collegium stays clear to all under Section 3.3, the following remain with the Provost: the voice under Section 4.3, the custody of the Great Seal, the conferral, withholding or revocation of ranks, the amendment of these Statutes, finances, the granting or revoking of Warrants, and the binding of the Collegium.
(b) A Pro-Chancellor holds a place of honour at convocations but no seat on the Academic Senate by virtue of the office alone. This does not affect any seat the Pro-Chancellor holds by virtue of substantive Fellowship under Section 4.4.
(c) A Pro-Chancellor holds no visitorial authority and does not act for or succeed the Provost. Succession is governed by Section 4.5 alone.
6. Countersignature Is Ceremonial. A diploma is valid on the signature and the Great Seal that Section 6.6(5) requires. The absence, delay or refusal of a countersignature, or the vacancy of the office of Pro-Chancellor, does not affect any rank or diploma, whether already conferred or later conferred.
7. Capacity and Costs. A Pro-Chancellor acts as an individual and not as an agent of any other body, and serves without stipend under Article X.
8. Presiding at Convocations. At any convocation the Provost may preside in person, delegate the presiding or the presentation of diplomas to the Secretary-General or a Pro-Chancellor, delegate the presentation of diplomas to a Legate under Section 6.9, or act as master of ceremonies. A rank is conferred by the Provost’s decree under the Great Seal, and the manner of presentation does not affect its validity.

Article IV

Perpetual Governance & Curial Offices

§ 4.1The Office of Founder, Provost & Prior-General

1. Vesting of the Office. The supreme executive governance, canonical protection, intellectual direction, and custodial ownership of the Collegium are vested in perpetuity in the office of Provost and Prior-General of the Collegium, which is held by the Founder, The Most Reverend Mar Elisha Thoma, for life, and thereafter by the Provost for the time being as Sections 4.5 and 11.3 provide, and subject to Sections 4.13 and 4.14.
2. Title and Styles. The Provost bears the official title of Provost and Prior-General, and bears the styles Chancellor and Director (the latter in external, secular, diplomatic and academic correspondence), as Section 3.4(1) provides.
3. Canonical Standing. Within the internal life of the Collegium, the Provost holds the canonical authority and immunity of an Ordinary and Abbot-Superior. This is an ecclesial canonical standing, ordered by the canon law of the Collegium. It is not a standing of the civil law and claims no exemption from it in any place.

During the foundational epoch of the Collegium, and whenever deemed expedient by the Provost, the following executive and academic offices are united ex officio in the person of the Provost, until such time as the Provost appoints deputy officers by formal decree:
1. Dean of Academic Studies (Magister Studiorum): Overseeing curriculum design, portfolio assessments, and the conferral of Licentiates and certificates. In these Statutes this officer is called the Dean of Studies.
2. Keeper of the Archives & Historiographer (Custos Archivorum): Managing the digital repositories and primary source collections of the Spruit-Bugge heritage.

§ 4.4The Academic Senate (Senatus Academicus)

1. Establishment & Character: The Academic Senate is the collegiate advisory body of the Herman Adrian Spruit Research Collegium. It represents the collective scholarly wisdom and contemplative experience of the society.
2. Composition & Automatic Seat:
Every substantive Fellow of the Collegium (FHASRC) holds an automatic seat on the Academic Senate ipso facto upon admission.
Honorary Fellows (HonFHASRC), Emeriti (FHASRC (Em.)), Licentiates (LHASRC), Associates (AHASRC), and Members (MHASRC) do not hold seats in the Academic Senate.
3. Convocation & Presider: The Provost and Prior-General serves ex officio as Presider of the Academic Senate. The Senate convenes virtually or in person solely upon the official summons and convocation of the Provost.
4. Tasks and Advice: The Senate has the tasks that Ordinance 2 sets out: peer review for the Journal, the reading of petitions through Review Panels, the sponsorship of candidates, and advice on research and archival work. Its advice on a petition for the Fellowship is given by a Review Panel of three, and is advice only. A rank is conferred only as Section 4.8(3) provides.
5. Non-Stipendiary & Voluntary Standing: All seats within the Academic Senate, all Fellowship ranks (FHASRC), and all editorial appointments within the Collegium are strictly honorary, voluntary, and non-stipendiary. Collegians serve in the spirit of sacred scholarship, Christian stewardship, and mutual intellectual advancement. Admission to the Fellowship or seating in the Senate creates no civil contract of employment, agency, partnership, or expectation of pecuniary remuneration.

§ 4.6The Secretary-General (Secretarius Generalis)

§ 4.11The Canons (Canonici)

1. Nature: A Canon is a Collegian whom the Provost appoints to give counsel and to keep the common prayer of the Collegium, and who is honoured by the title. The office is one of counsel and not of governance. It is voluntary and non-stipendiary under Article X.
2. Who May Be a Canon: A Collegian in good standing who holds any rank under Section 5.2 or honour under Section 5.5, whether a bishop, priest, deacon or layperson. Ordination is not required, and a layperson is styled Lay Canon.
4. What a Canon Does: A Canon does the following, and nothing more:
(a) gives counsel to the Provost, when asked, on doctrine, liturgy, rite, discipline, the Statutes and the Ordinances, and the Provost’s vision and mission;
(b) reviews in writing, at the Provost’s request, the Rites, Study Packs, statements of faith and drafts of the Statutes and Ordinances;
(c) keeps the Collegium’s common prayer, leading or arranging the offices of prayer and memorial that the Provost sets; and
(d) gives spiritual and pastoral care to the Collegians whom the Provost sends.
5. Order and Ministry: An ordained Canon may do the acts that the Canon’s own order allows, at the Collegium’s gatherings when the Provost asks. Those acts come from the order and not from the Canonry. A Lay Canon serves in counsel and prayer, and sacramental acts are those of the ordained.
6. Counsel, Not Governance: A Canon serves as a counsellor, in the manner of the canons of a cathedral, who sits at the Provost’s side and speaks when asked. In particular:
(a) Governance belongs to the Provost, the voice under Section 4.3 to the Provost and the bodies that Section 4.3 names, the designation of a successor to the Provost alone, ranks and Warrants to those whom Section 4.8(3) names, and the funds of the Collegium to the Provost and a Civil Steward under Section 10.6.
(b) A seat in the Elder Council or the Senate comes from its own Section and not from the Canonry.
(c) The Canon’s counsel is offered freely, and the Provost may take it or leave it.
(d) Whatever a Canon does beyond this section is the Canon’s own act and not the Collegium’s.
7. Not a Body: The Canons form no chapter or council. If the Provost convenes them together, they are advisory only.

Certified copies are required for documents, and a record of ministry is supported by a referee, a community or an institution that can confirm it. 5. No Fee: No fee is charged for an RPEL application or its assessment, consistent with Section 5.1.
Article VI

Academic Jurisdiction, Licentiates & Certifications

§ 6.1Canonical Authority to Confer Ecclesiastical Awards

In accordance with ancient ecclesiastical custom, the Collegium exercises its inherent right under the archiepiscopal Decree by which it was erected and the mandate of the Provost-Founder and of each Provost after the Founder to examine candidates and confer ecclesiastical diplomas, Licentiates, and practitioner certificates.

§ 6.2Nature & Examination Modality of the Ecclesiastical Licentiate

1. British Examination Convention. The HASRC Licentiate follows the British examination convention, in which a licentiate is a qualification conferred by a learned or professional body on the strength of examination, and so grants a formal licence (licentia docendi) to teach within a sacred discipline.
2. Standard. It is an intensive, self-paced modular examination programme, assessed at a standard of comprehension rigour comparable to upper-division undergraduate or early graduate theological inquiry.
3. What Is Examined. The Collegium examines scholarship about mystical, metaphysical and sacramental subjects, and trains students for ministry through study.
4. Not a Certificate of Attainment. It does not examine, certify or guarantee any person’s spiritual attainment, and no rank, including the Licentiate in Applied Metaphysical Science, is a certificate of spiritual attainment.
5. Syllabus. Following the collegiate examining board model, each Licentiate syllabus comprises six (6) dedicated Subject Modules and one (1) major Capstone Treatise.
6. Folio. Upon free enrolment, candidates receive the complete digital module pack and prepare their Consolidated Examination Folio externally, at their own pace, within the window that Ordinance 7 sets and to the word counts that Ordinance 5 sets.

§ 6.3The Four Specialist Licentiate Programmes & Syllabus Architecture

1. Two Stages. In each stream, the six modules form two stages. Modules 1, 2, and 3 are the fixed foundational sequence.
2. Associate by Route A. A candidate who completes them and is examined under Route A of Ordinance 5 is conferred the rank of Associate (AHASRC) in that stream, and may rest there.
3. Licentiate. Modules 4, 5, and 6 with the Capstone Treatise then complete the Licentiate.
4. Associates by Route B or C. An Associate admitted under Route B or Route C does not take the foundational modules to enter. Such an Associate takes no modular credit toward the Licentiate by reason of the Associate rank, and enters the Licentiate by a route of Ordinance 5.
5. The Four Streams. The four specialist streams are the Licentiate of Sacramental Ministry (L.S.Min), the Licentiate of Metaphysical Theology (L.Mphy.Th.), the Licentiate of Applied Metaphysical Science (L.AMsc.) and the Licentiate in Contemporary Spirituality (L.C.Sp.). Their modules and Capstone Treatises are set out in Ordinance 8.
6. Revision. They may be revised by Ordinance, but every stream keeps six Modules and one Capstone Treatise.

§ 6.3.1Non-Automaticity of Holy Orders (Ordination Disclaimer)

1. Preparation, Not Ordination. Completion of the Licentiate of Sacramental Ministry (L.S.Min) or any collegiate rank within the HASRC certifies academic, theoretical, and spiritual preparation, but does not confer, mandate, or guarantee ordination to the Diaconate, Priesthood, or Episcopate within the Australian Church of Antioch or any Independent Sacramental jurisdiction.
2. Ordination an Independent Act. Sacramental ordination remains an independent act of episcopal grace and discernment, requiring the candidate to separately satisfy their ordaining Ordinary and Synod’s canonical prerequisites, including a statutory police background check, character vetting, hands-on liturgical practicum (or recorded Mass demonstration), and canonical obedience.

§ 6.4Open Public Certifications (Non-Collegiate Track)

1. Open Programmes. The Collegium may from time to time develop and offer open public programmes and certificates of its own design, as educational and spiritual-formation outreach.
2. Independent Assessment. Each is announced with its own description and is assessed independently of the rank ladder.
3. Not a Prerequisite. No such programme is a prerequisite for any collegiate rank, and none is a medical, psychological, counselling or other professional qualification.
4. Offering. Any programme is offered free of charge or by voluntary offering, consistent with Article X, and a fee may be introduced only under Section 10.5(2).

§ 6.5Candidacy Windows & Collegiate Review (De Temporibus Candidaturae)

1. Windows: Candidacy runs within the maximum windows that Ordinance 7 sets. The programmes are self-paced: there is no minimum candidacy period, and a candidate may submit whenever the work is complete. A candidacy not submitted by the close of its window, including any extension, lapses without prejudice, and the candidate may re-petition.
2. Dual Assessment: Every Consolidated Examination Folio, Capstone Treatise, Research Monograph, and portfolio is read by at least two (2) assessors, the Dean of Studies being one. Each assessor discloses any conflict of interest before reading, and a conflicted assessor stands aside. For every Fellowship petition the two assessors are the Dean of Studies and a second assessor, who is a Fellow, an officer of the Collegium or an invited reader, and a third member of the same description joins them as a Review Panel under Ordinance 2(2).
3. Service Aim: The Chancery aims to issue a decision within ninety (90) days of a complete submission. This is a service aim only and creates no entitlement to any outcome.

§ 6.6Integrity of Awards (De Integritate Graduum)

1. Public Register of Awards: The Chancery keeps a Public Register of Awards recording each numbered certificate, the rank, the post-nominal, and the date of conferral. A holder may ask for personal details to be withheld, in which case the Register shows the certificate number and rank only. The Register is published online and may be searched by certificate number, so that anyone can verify an award.
2. Earned and Honorary Distinctions: The earned ranks of Associate, Licentiate, and Fellow are conferred only on assessment, save for the Founding Fellowship and the Provost’s Fellowship, which are substantive. Honorary, Emeritus, and diplomatic honours under Section 5.5 (other than the Founding Fellowship and the Provost’s Fellowship) are conferred by decree, are always designated honoris causa on the certificate and in the Register, and confer no examination-based standing.
3. No Relaxation of Standards: The absence of fees relaxes no academic standard. No rank is conferred on request, on enrolment, or on the giving of an offering alone.
4. Revocation for Falsification: An award obtained by falsification, plagiarism, or misrepresentation was never lawfully conferred. Such conduct constitutes grave canonical delinquency for the purposes of Section 5.6 and Section 11.4. Withdrawal of the award is recorded in the Register after written notice to the holder and an opportunity to respond.
5. Signature of Diplomas. A diploma is signed under the Great Seal as follows:
(a) Seal and Title. A diploma is valid on the signature of the person who confers the rank under Section 4.8(3) and the Great Seal. That signature and seal give the rank its perpetual title under Section 5.6.
(b) In the Ordinary Course. The Provost, as Chancellor, signs alone, or with the Pro-Chancellor when one is in office, in accordance with Section 3.4.

§ 6.8Reasons, Complaints, Review, Appeals & Withdrawal

1. Written Reasons: The Chancery gives written reasons for any decision that refuses, suspends, withdraws or revokes a rank, an award, good standing or a Warrant.
2. Complaints: Any person may complain to the Chancery in writing about the conduct of the Collegium, an assessor, an officer or a chartered branch. The Chancery acknowledges a complaint within fourteen (14) days and aims to reply within sixty (60) days. These are service aims only.
3. Request for Review: A person affected by a decision under item 1 may, within thirty (30) days of receiving the reasons, ask in writing for review.
4. Appeal Panel: Review is made by a Panel of three (3) persons named by the Provost for the matter, none of whom took part in the decision under review or has a conflict of interest. Where practicable, at least one is a person from outside the Collegium. The Panel may uphold the decision, send the matter back for fresh assessment by different assessors, or recommend another outcome.
5. Decision: The Panel reports to the Provost in writing. The Provost decides, gives written reasons and, if not following the Panel’s recommendation, says why. This Section does not limit Section 4.3.
6. Scope: Review looks at fairness of procedure, conflict of interest, error of fact and compliance with these Statutes. Disagreement alone with an assessor’s academic judgement is not a ground of review.
7. Interim Measures: A request for review does not stop an interim suspension under Section 1.4(7) or Section 5.9(3)(c).
8. Confidence and Records: Complaints and reviews are handled in confidence, and their records are kept under Section 9.2.
9. No Claim; Withdrawal: Membership of the Collegium, and the ranks, awards and Warrants it gives, are not held under any contract with the Collegium. A Collegian who does not accept these Statutes or a decision under this Section may withdraw by written notice, and withdrawal ends every claim against the Collegium. A rank conferred remains valid under Section 5.6. Review under this Section is the Collegium’s own remedy, and this item does not limit any right that the law of the land gives a person to go to the civil authorities, as Section 1.4(9) provides.

§ 6.9Presentation of Awards (Legates)

1. Conferral and Presentation: A rank is conferred by decree of the Provost under the Great Seal. The presentation of a diploma is ceremonial, and the manner of presentation does not affect validity (Section 3.4(8)).
2. Who May Present: Only the Provost, an Acting Provost, a Coadjutor Provost, a Provost-Designate, the Secretary-General, a Pro-Chancellor, or a Legate of the Provost may present a diploma.
3. The Legate: A Legate is a Collegian appointed by the Provost or, under Section 4.6, by the Secretary-General, by Letter of Delegation for a stated occasion, which may be as short as one day. Who may be appointed, the rank each may present, dress and the record are set out in Ordinance 10.

2. Collegiate Peer Review: Every submission is peer reviewed as follows:
(a) Every submission undergoes editorial screening by the Editor-in-Chief, followed by review by at least two independent reviewers, drawn from the Fellows (FHASRC) or from invited external scholars with relevant expertise.

(i) fit with the research streams of Section 7.2;

(iii) the soundness of the method and the use of evidence and sources;

(v) research ethics, including consent and confidentiality in case studies and the absence of plagiarism.

Members (MHASRC) are Collegians only for the purposes stated in the decree that activates that tier under Section 5.3.
4. Dean of Studies: the officer named in Section 4.2(1). Wherever these Statutes or the Ordinances refer to the Dean of Studies, the Dean of Academic Studies (Magister Studiorum) is meant.

6. Grave Canonical Delinquency: (a) falsification, plagiarism or misrepresentation in obtaining or using an award; (b) serious abuse, exploitation or fraud affecting another person, established after fair enquiry; or (c) knowing misuse of the Great Seal, the name, or the ranks of the Collegium to deceive. Disagreement, dissent or private belief is never delinquency. Any finding follows written notice and an opportunity to respond, and is open to review under Section 6.8.
8. Interpretation: headings are for convenience and do not limit the text. Where these Statutes refer to the Registry or the Roll in the sense of records, the Register is meant.
9. Supremacy of the Statutes: These Statutes are the supreme internal law of the Collegium. Warrants, the Model Constitution, the Ordinances, Study Packs, every local constitution and every other instrument in the Schedule of Instruments are subordinate to them and are read consistently with them. Where the Statutes are silent, the Provost decides in the light of their spirit and of the general principles of canon law received in the Catholic, Old Catholic and Anglican heritage. The canons and regulations of ACOA or SMDS bind the Collegium only so far as the Provost accepts them by sealed instrument or Concordat.
13. Decree of the Provost: a written act of the Provost under the Great Seal. 16. Warrant: the Warrant of Constitution granted by the Provost under Section 1.4(1), by which a Chapter or Oratory is chartered.
17. Branch: a Chapter, an Oratory or a Combined Foundation chartered under a Warrant.
20. Canon: a Collegian whom the Provost appoints under Section 4.11 to give counsel and to keep the common prayer of the Collegium, whether ordained or lay. A Canon counsels and does not govern.

§ 11.5The Ordinances (Ordinationes)

1. Purpose. The Provost may, by Decree of the Provost, make, vary and repeal Ordinances for the rules of procedure, the requirements, curricula, regalia and other matters that these Statutes commit to them, so that these Statutes need not be changed as times and needs change.
2. Subordinate to the Statutes. An Ordinance has force only so far as it is consistent with these Statutes. If an Ordinance conflicts with them, the Statutes prevail and the Ordinance is void to that extent. An Ordinance cannot enlarge the powers of any body or officer beyond those given in these Statutes (Section 4.8).
3. Publication and Effect. An Ordinance is published on the Collegium’s website with its date, and takes effect on that date or on a later date it names. A change to the requirements for a rank applies to petitions filed after it takes effect. A candidate already in candidacy may complete under the requirements in force when the petition was filed.
4. Protected Rights. No Ordinance revokes, diminishes or alters any rank, diploma or Warrant lawfully conferred (Sections 5.6 and 11.1(4)).
