The Theological "Why"

Understanding the Foundations of Our Charism

The Licentiate of Metaphysical Theology (L.Mphy.Th.) is intended for the theologian, mystic, clergy, or author seeking a profound academic and contemplative understanding of the wisdom and historical context of our spiritual tradition. While our practical tracks emphasize ministerial mechanics, this stream investigates the theoretical and historical "why" behind the Independent Sacramental Movement.

Candidates study the convergent historical succession lines of Antioch, the Desert Fathers, Christian Platonism, Meister Eckhart, and the groundbreaking meta-Catholic universalism of Patriarch Herman Adrian Spruit, Matriarch Meri Spruit, and Archbishop Frank R. Bugge.

It grants the Collegium’s ecclesiastical licence to teach (licentia docendi) within a sacred discipline. See the legal notice below.

Rigorous, Non-Dogmatic Inquiry

Because the Collegium operates as an ecclesiastical research sodality rather than a sectarian synod, candidates are subjected to no dogmatic theological loyalty oaths. Advancing through the curriculum requires demonstrating critical engagement, bibliographic transparency, and intellectual integrity.

The program examines rigorous academic scholarship and contemplative literature; no rank is conferred as a certificate of personal spiritual attainment.

Canonical Pathways

Three Routes to Admission

A candidate fulfils one of three assessed routes under the Foundational Statutes and Ordinance 5. Enrolment, examination, assessment and conferral are free of charge under every route.

Route A

Full Curricular Track

Open to all qualified scholars, authors, and mystics. Candidates complete the six Core Subject Modules and an 8,000-word Capstone Treatise within a 12-month self-paced window, with a progress notice in the 9th month. Associates admitted under Route A complete Modules 4–6 and the Treatise.

Deliverable: 6 Essays + Capstone Treatise
Route B

Ministerial & APEL Portfolio

Open to clergy serving in apostolic, autocephalous, or independent sacramental traditions, being a Permanent Deacon, a Priest or a Bishop. The Ministerial Portfolio Dossier records at least five years of service with verified lineage documentation and is assessed in place of the six modules.

Deliverable: Dossier + 8,000-Word Treatise
Route C

Graduate Degree Bridge

Open to holders of an earned Master’s degree. An accredited Master’s waives three core modules (50%); an independent or unaccredited seminary Master’s earns APEL standing for up to three modules after transcript audit by the Dean of Studies. The remaining modules or the Capstone Treatise are then completed.

Deliverable: Transcripts + Remaining Modules / Treatise
Curricular Architecture

The Six Subject Modules

Structured into a 3-module foundational sequence leading to the Associate rank (AHASRC / A.Mphy.Th.), followed by three upper-division licentiate modules and the culminating Capstone Treatise.

Stage I • Foundation Sequence

Associate Level • AHASRC (A.Mphy.Th.)

Modules 1 to 3
MODULE 01

The Legacy of Patriarch Herman Adrian Spruit & Matriarch Meri Spruit

The foundational history, philosophy, and writings of Patriarch Herman and Matriarch Meri Spruit, focusing on meta-Catholicity, sacramental justice, open communion, and the synthesis of classical liturgy with New Thought.

Assessed: 2,000-word Module Essay
MODULE 02

The Independent Sacramental Movement: History, Lineages & Autocephaly

Historical origins, lineages, and autocephalous developments of the modern ISM. Traces Syro-Antiochene, Old Catholic, and Anglican lines of apostolic succession, independent jurisdiction, and validity.

Assessed: 2,000-word Module Essay
MODULE 03

Esoteric Christianity & Primordial Wisdom Traditions

The inner stream of Christian spirituality, Hermetic roots, Christian Gnosis, esoteric sacraments, and the convergence of Christian mysticism with the perennial philosophy.

Assessed: 2,000-word Module Essay
Intermediate Associate Exit: Completion of Modules 1–3 and a 2,000-word Synthesis Theological Paper qualifies for Associate rank (AHASRC / A.Mphy.Th.). 8,000 Words Total
Stage II • Licentiate Completion

Licentiate Level • LHASRC (L.Mphy.Th.)

Modules 4 to 6 + Treatise
MODULE 04

Christian Mysticism & The Contemplative Tradition

The apophatic and kataphatic traditions in Christian mysticism, examining the Desert Fathers, Pseudo-Dionysius, Meister Eckhart, the Cloud of Unknowing, and unitive contemplative prayer.

Assessed: 2,000-word Module Essay
MODULE 05

The Christian Creed: Esoteric, Symbolic & Metaphysical Exegesis

A profound metaphysical deconstruction of historical creeds (Apostles' and Nicene), uncovering their cosmic, allegorical, and subtle energy significance beyond rigid dogmatic literalism.

Assessed: 2,000-word Module Essay
MODULE 06

Biblical Metaphors, Sacred Symbolism & New Testament Context

Contemporary literary, historical, and esoteric exegesis of canonical and extra-canonical scriptures, focusing on metaphysical keys, sacred symbolism, and spiritual awakening.

Assessed: 2,000-word Module Essay
Scholarly Capstone

Examination Protocol & Academic Apparatus

Candidates bundle their deliverables into one Consolidated Examination Folio, a single PDF transmitted to the Chancery with government photo identification and a Declaration of Academic Integrity. The work culminates in an 8,000-word Capstone Treatise.

The 8,000-Word Capstone Treatise

Written in one of the five approved domains of the L.Mphy.Th. Study Pack: an abstract of up to 300 words, then five chapters in the customary theological thesis structure, referenced in APA 7th Edition style:

  • 1. Introduction & Research Question
  • 2. Review of Literature
  • 3. Method & Sources
  • 4. Analysis & Discussion
  • 5. Conclusion

Must cite a minimum of twenty-five primary, patristic, archival, or peer-reviewed sources. Word counts exclude the title page, abstract, reference list and appendices.

Evaluation & Collegiate Review

Every folio and treatise is read by at least two assessors, the Dean of Studies being one. Each discloses any conflict of interest before reading, and the Chancery aims to decide within 90 days of a complete submission.

  • • Prior Approval: Candidates submit a 300-word topic proposal and provisional bibliography to the Dean of Studies before writing.
  • • Charism Engagement: Where Ordinance 5 requires it (Route B), the treatise examines at least one of the five pillars of the Spruitian Charism. Assessors judge the quality of the engagement, not agreement with it.
  • • Fresh Work: The treatise is newly written, or the substantial completion of earlier research never submitted for an award or published. Earlier drafts are disclosed, and every treatise is checked for originality.
  • • Desk-Reject Rule: Incomplete folios, works more than 10% under the word count, works over a stated maximum, and submissions without a formal reference apparatus are returned unassessed. Licentiates have priority consideration for monograph publication in the Journal of Inner Spirituality (JOIS).

Canonical Governance & Civil Disclosure

Official Ecclesiastical & Legal Disclosure Notice

The Herman Adrian Spruit Research Collegium (HASRC) is an autonomous, not-for-profit ecclesiastical learned society, research scriptorium, and educational ministry, erected by Archiepiscopal Decree as a voluntary religious sodality under the canonical jurisdiction of its Founder and Provost, operating in sacramental fellowship with the Australian Church of Antioch (ACOA), and open to concordats and articulation agreements with other churches and schools of sacred learning, which take effect when signed by both parties and are published in its Register of Concordats and Articulations.

HASRC is strictly a religious and metaphysical institute of sacred learning, not a secular university or civil college. Its curricula, certificates, Licentiates, and Fellowship ranks are exclusively ecclesiastical and non-secular in character, conferred under the inherent apostolic and canonical authority of the Provost and the Collegium for ministerial formation, spiritual progression, and theological scholarship. HASRC does not claim, nor does it seek, secular civil governmental accreditation. Its credentials are designed for ministerial and sacred-scholarly purposes, not for secular public-sector employment or civil university credit transfer, and are issued for use within the canonical purview of the Collegium, its affiliated ecclesiastical bodies, and the wider Independent Sacramental Movement.

Nothing taught, offered or celebrated by the Collegium or any chartered branch is medical, psychological, counselling or other regulated professional advice or treatment, and no credential of the Collegium qualifies its holder to practise any regulated profession.
