The Theological "Why"

Understanding the Foundations of Our Charism

The Licentiate of Metaphysical Theology (L.Mphy.Th.) is intended for the theologian, mystic, clergy, or seeker who seeks a profound academic and contemplative understanding of the wisdom and context of our spiritual tradition. While our practical programs emphasize ministerial mechanics and practitioner arts, this track investigates the theoretical and historical "why" behind the Independent Sacramental Movement.

It is particularly suited for authors, writers, teachers, and those focused on self-development who wish to gain a deep conceptual grasp of our heritage. The curriculum encompasses Antiochian succession history, comparative Christian mysticism, early Gnostic and Hermetic roots, and the groundbreaking meta-Catholic universalism of Patriarch Herman Adrian Spruit.

This program is earned by completing a curriculum of required reading, submitting analytical spiritual-theological essays, and producing a final research dissertation that demonstrates a comprehensive grasp of one's spiritual theology.

Rigorous Yet Non-Dogmatic Inquiry

Students engage sacred scriptures, church history, and mystical philosophy with academic freedom, intellectual honesty, and deep contemplative respect.

Scholarly Objectives

Program Learning Outcomes

Upon earning the L.Mphy.Th. credential, the graduate will be able to:

01

Examine the historical genesis, catholicity, and apostolic lines of the Independent Sacramental Movement with scholarly authority.

02

Analyze the unique theological synthesis of Patriarch Herman Adrian Spruit and Archbishop Frank Bugge within twentieth-century non-papal Catholicism.

03

Deconstruct biblical texts through both contemporary historical exegesis and ancient allegorical/esoteric hermeneutics.

04

Situate Antiochian mysticism within the broader continuum of Christian mysticism, apophatic theology, and perennial philosophy.

05

Articulate an integrative, non-creedal spiritual theology addressing contemporary ethical and interfaith dilemmas.

06

Produce an original theological dissertation demonstrating rigorous research methodology in APA 7th style.

Curriculum Framework

72 Credit Hours Syllabus Architecture

Organized across 4 core research clusters (36 Credits), a spiritual-theological essay portfolio (18 Credits), and a final research dissertation (18 Credits).

I. Advanced Theological & Historical Coursework

36 Credits Total

Cluster A: ISM Ecclesiology & Antiochian Lineages

9 Credits
Historical Lineages (3 Cr): Origins of the Independent Sacramental Movement, Vilatte successions, and lines of convergence.
The Spruit Legacy (3 Cr): Patriarch Herman A. Spruit's meta-Catholic ecclesiology, sacramental justice, and open communion.
Celtic & Druidic Lines (3 Cr): Archbishop Frank Bugge, Archdruid Cluthin, and unbroken female lines of apostolic succession.

Cluster B: Christian Mysticism & Esoteric Theology

9 Credits
Comparative Mysticism (3 Cr): The apophatic way, Pseudo-Dionysius, Meister Eckhart, and the Desert Fathers.
Esoteric Christianity (3 Cr): Hidden Gnostic traditions, Christian Hermeticism, and the cosmic Christos principle.
Metaphysics of Faith (3 Cr): Unitive consciousness, mystical union, and the reality of the divine essence.

Cluster C: Sacramental & Creeds Metaphysics

9 Credits
Sacramental Science (3 Cr): The operative energetic mechanics of the Eucharist and clairvoyant liturgical observation.
Esoteric Creeds (3 Cr): Deconstructing traditional dogma into cosmic truths: solar Christology, Monad, and regeneration.
Liturgical Theology (3 Cr): Evolution of Western rites, the Liberal Catholic liturgy, and contemporary Antiochian forms.

Cluster D: Biblical Hermeneutics & Symbolism

9 Credits
Contemporary Exegesis (3 Cr): Critical historical, literary, and theological analysis of canonical biblical texts.
Biblical Metaphors (3 Cr): Allegorical, kabbalistic, and metaphysical interpretations of scriptures.
New Testament Origins (3 Cr): Sociopolitical contexts, canonical development, and early extra-canonical literature.

II. Spiritual-Theological Essay Portfolio

18 Credits Required

Candidates submit three (3) peer-reviewed academic essays (3,000 words each, 6 credits each) exploring the theological foundation and practical charism of the tradition:

Essay 1: Ecclesiological Synthesis A critical paper on how Antiochian sacramentalism reconciles traditional apostolic succession with radical inclusion and modern metaphysics.
Essay 2: Mystical Christology A comparative analysis of historical Christian mysticism and Patriarch Spruit's formulation of the living Christic reality.
Essay 3: Operative Liturgics A philosophical investigation into the science of ritual efficacy, subtle energy mechanics, and modern contemplative consciousness.

III. Final Theological Dissertation

18 Credits Required

The culminating requirement is a formal academic research thesis (12,000 to 18,000 words) demonstrating a comprehensive and original grasp of one's spiritual theology.

Supervision & Direction: Undertaken under the advisement of the Director of Studies or an appointed Senior Fellow.
Journal Archiving: High-merit dissertations are recommended for publication as featured monographs in the Journal of Inner Spirituality (JOIS).
Capstone Research

The L.Mphy.Th. Thesis Standards

The dissertation represents the candidate's formal contribution to the scholarly and theological corpus of the Independent Sacramental Movement. The thesis must present an original, well-researched argument exploring theological history, sacramental philosophy, or esoteric Christian hermeneutics.

  • Word Length: 12,000 – 18,000 words (exclusive of abstract, table of contents, and references).
  • Citation Standard: Full compliance with APA 7th Edition style (or Chicago Notes and Bibliography format for historical treatises).
  • Review Process: Evaluated by the Director of Studies and an external reader from our episcopal/academic fellowship.
Standards of Entry

Admission Criteria & Prerequisites

Academic Background

  • Bachelor’s degree in theology, philosophy, religious studies, literature, or equivalent liberal arts.
  • Candidates without a formal degree must demonstrate comprehension equivalent to a 3rd-year Bachelor of Arts.
  • Demonstrated writing ability through previous articles, books, homilies, or theological reflections.

Prior Learning & Fellow Standing

Prior accredited graduate studies in religion or published works in ecclesiastical history may be considered for up to 30% Prior Learning Assessment (PLA) transfer credits.

Post-Nominal & Learned Society: Graduates earn the post-nominal L.Mphy.Th. and receive fast-track recommendation for appointment as Associate Fellow (Assc.F.HASRC) or Research Fellow (Res.F.HASRC).
