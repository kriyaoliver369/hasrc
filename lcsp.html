Pastoral Formation Through Study

Inclusive Pastoral Care for a Pluralist World

The Licentiate in Contemporary Spirituality (L.C.Sp.) is an integrative pastoral and mentoring track for spiritual directors, pastoral companions, and community facilitators. It examines the ethics of pastoral ministry and spiritual direction, interfaith ministry and comparative religion, and the theology of radical inclusion.

Candidates then turn to pastoral guidance on marriage, family and relationships, to conflict management and community peacebuilding, and to organisational leadership and sacred administration, the practical matters that a mentor or community leader meets in daily service.

It grants the Collegium’s ecclesiastical licence to teach (licentia docendi) within a sacred discipline. See the legal notice below.

Rigorous, Non-Dogmatic Inquiry

The Collegium is an ecclesiastical research sodality rather than a sectarian synod, so candidates take no dogmatic loyalty oath. Advancing through the curriculum requires critical engagement, bibliographic transparency, and intellectual integrity.

Study, Not Clinical Qualification

The L.C.Sp. examines scholarship and trains students for ministry through study. It is not a medical, psychological, or counselling qualification, and no rank is a certificate of personal spiritual attainment.

Scholarly Competencies

What Candidates Demonstrate

Through the six module essays and the Capstone Treatise, candidates show that they can:

01

Analyse the ethics, boundaries and safeguarding questions of pastoral ministry and spiritual direction.

02

Compare the world’s religions with scholarly care and reflect on the practice of interfaith ministry.

03

Engage queer theologies and the theology of radical inclusive ministry with evidence and argument.

04

Frame pastoral guidance on marriage, family and relationships within a sound theological and ethical framework.

05

Study conflict management and community peacebuilding, and the governance and administration of sacred communities.

06

Write a referenced 8,000-word Capstone Treatise that synthesises contemporary spirituality and pastoral practice.

Canonical Pathways

Three Routes to Admission

A candidate fulfils one of three assessed routes under the Foundational Statutes and Ordinance 5. Enrolment, examination, assessment and conferral are free of charge under every route.

Route A

Full Curricular Examination

Free enrolment and self-paced completion of the six Core Subject Modules and the Capstone Treatise within a 12-month candidacy window, with a progress notice in the 9th month. Associates of the same stream complete Modules 4, 5 and 6 and the Treatise.

Deliverable: 6 Essays + Capstone Treatise
Route B

Ministerial & APEL Portfolio

Open to clergy serving in apostolic, autocephalous, or independent sacramental traditions, being a Permanent Deacon, a Priest or a Bishop. The Ministerial Portfolio Dossier records at least five years of service and is assessed in place of the six modules.

Deliverable: Dossier + 8,000-Word Treatise
Route C

Graduate Degree Bridge

Open to holders of an earned Master’s degree. An accredited Master’s waives three modules (50%); an independent or unaccredited seminary Master’s earns APEL standing for up to three modules, after transcript audit by the Dean of Studies.

Deliverable: Transcripts + Remaining Modules / Treatise

Prior experience and learning is recognised only where Ordinance 5 allows it, never for more modules than it allows, and never for the Capstone Treatise.

Scholarly Capstone

Examination Protocol & Academic Apparatus

Candidates bundle their deliverables into one Consolidated Examination Folio, a single PDF transmitted to the Chancery with government photo identification and a Declaration of Academic Integrity. The work culminates in an 8,000-word Capstone Treatise.

The 8,000-Word Capstone Treatise

An abstract of up to 300 words, then five chapters in the customary theological thesis structure, referenced in APA 7th Edition style:

  • 1. Introduction & Research Question
  • 2. Review of Literature
  • 3. Method & Sources
  • 4. Analysis & Discussion
  • 5. Conclusion

Must cite a minimum of twenty-five primary, patristic, archival, or peer-reviewed sources. Word counts exclude the title page, abstract, reference list and appendices.

Evaluation & Collegiate Review

Every folio and treatise is read by at least two assessors, the Dean of Studies being one. Each discloses any conflict of interest before reading, and the Chancery aims to decide within 90 days of a complete submission.

  • • Prior Approval: Before writing, candidates submit a 300-word proposal and provisional bibliography to the Dean of Studies.
  • • Fresh Work: The treatise is newly written, or the substantial completion of earlier research never submitted for an award or published. Earlier drafts are disclosed, and every treatise is checked for originality.
  • • Desk-Reject Rule: Incomplete folios, works more than 10% under the word count, works over a stated maximum, and submissions without a formal reference apparatus are returned unassessed. Subjective journaling, devotional piety, pulpit homilies and unreferenced speculation are not assessed.
  • • Charism Engagement: Where Ordinance 5 requires it (Route B), the treatise examines at least one of the five pillars of the Spruitian Charism. Assessors judge the quality of the engagement, not agreement with it.

Candidacy Windows

The Licentiate window is a maximum of twelve months, with a progress notice in the 9th month. The programme is self-paced with no minimum period. One extension of up to six months may be granted on written petition to the Dean of Studies before the window closes. A candidacy not submitted in time lapses without prejudice, and the candidate may re-petition.

Publication: Licentiates have priority consideration for monograph publication in the Journal of Inner Spirituality (JOIS), which publishes under the Collegium’s peer-review and open-access policy.

Standing & Progression

Rights of the Licentiate

Post-Nominals & Privileges

  • The collegiate post-nominal LHASRC, used alone or beside the discipline designation L.C.Sp.
  • Right to wear the Licentiate academic regalia at ecclesiastical and collegiate convocations.
  • Eligibility to petition an institution with an articulation agreement in force for advanced standing toward a master’s-level ministerial degree, on the terms in the Register of Concordats and Articulations.
  • The prerogative to sponsor and recommend candidates for the Associate rank.

Onward to Fellowship

The Licentiate does not lead automatically to Fellow (FHASRC). A holder may petition under Route D, the Discipline Fellowship by Research Monograph (18,000 to 25,000 words, defended, within twelve months of filing), and on ratification is styled F.C.Sp. A Licentiate may also pursue Fellowship under Route A (the JOIS research track).

The rank is recorded in the Public Register of Awards, where anyone can verify it by certificate number.

Canonical Governance & Civil Disclosure

Official Ecclesiastical & Legal Disclosure Notice

The Herman Adrian Spruit Research Collegium (HASRC) is an autonomous, not-for-profit ecclesiastical learned society, research scriptorium, and educational ministry, erected by Archiepiscopal Decree as a voluntary religious sodality under the canonical jurisdiction of its Founder and Provost, operating in sacramental fellowship with the Australian Church of Antioch (ACOA), and open to concordats and articulation agreements with other churches and schools of sacred learning, which take effect when signed by both parties and are published in its Register of Concordats and Articulations.

HASRC is strictly a religious and metaphysical institute of sacred learning, not a secular university or civil college. Its curricula, certificates, Licentiates, and Fellowship ranks are exclusively ecclesiastical and non-secular in character, conferred under the inherent apostolic and canonical authority of the Provost and the Collegium for ministerial formation, spiritual progression, and theological scholarship. HASRC does not claim, nor does it seek, secular civil governmental accreditation. Its credentials are designed for ministerial and sacred-scholarly purposes, not for secular public-sector employment or civil university credit transfer, and are issued for use within the canonical purview of the Collegium, its affiliated ecclesiastical bodies, and the wider Independent Sacramental Movement.

Nothing taught, offered or celebrated by the Collegium or any chartered branch is medical, psychological, counselling or other regulated professional advice or treatment, and no credential of the Collegium qualifies its holder to practise any regulated profession. Completion of a Licentiate certifies academic and spiritual preparation only and does not confer, mandate, or guarantee ordination.
