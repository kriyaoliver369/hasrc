De Capitulis et Oratoriis

Three ways to gather

Those who chiefly wish to study petition for a Chapter. Those who chiefly wish to pray petition for an Oratory. Those who wish both may be chartered together under one Warrant. Chapters and Oratories stand as equals.

What would your gathering be for?

A fellowship of the mind

Collegiate Chapter

Capitulum Academicum

For colloquia, for study of the Antiochian heritage and the Spruitian Charism, for meditation and for reunions. Led by a Chapter Warden, lay or ordained.

  • Study circles and colloquia
  • Reading the Collegium’s curricular materials together
  • Visiting speakers arranged through the Chancery
Led by a Chapter Warden
A fellowship of the heart

Oratory

Oratorium

For common prayer, meditation, healing prayer and the Collegium’s Order of Prayer. On appointed Eucharistic Days, the Holy Eucharist is celebrated by an invited priest.

  • The Order of Prayer, freely used by anyone
  • The ACOA Rite for the Eucharist
  • An optional Chaplain, an ordained Priest or Prelate

Self-governed, in communion

The life of a branch

Each branch governs its own local life, from programme and schedule to hospitality and study themes, under its own Constitution adopted from the Chancery’s Model Constitution. There is no regional or national hierarchy in between. You speak directly to the Chancery.

Your Warden

Every branch is presided over by a Warden, lay or ordained, who holds the level of Associate, Licentiate or Fellow. The first Warden is named in your petition and confirmed by the Warrant. Later Wardens are elected by the members and confirmed by the Provost. The Provost cannot impose a Warden the members have not chosen.

Your Chaplain

An Oratory may also have a Chaplain, an ordained Priest or Prelate who is a Collegian in good standing, for its sacramental and pastoral work. The Chaplain is named in the petition or later chosen by the members and confirmed by the Provost.

The medallion of office

At every regular assembly the Warden wears the medallion over ordinary dress, casual or formal. Full gown and hood are for solemn occasions, such as a convocation or the Feast of Holy Sophia, or when the Warden calls a dressed assembly.

What the Chancery gives every branch

  • The Model Constitution to adopt as your own
  • A listing on the Collegium’s website
  • Guidance on regalia
  • The medallion of office for your Warden (and Chaplain, if any)
  • Access to curricular materials and visiting speakers

Senior Warden & Senior Chaplain

After five years of cumulative active service in good standing, the Provost recognises a Warden or Chaplain as a Senior Warden or Senior Chaplain. It is an honour held for life, not a level. It carries the one-band epitoge as the symbol of long ministry and a place on the Roll of Voters. The title belongs to the holder, and goes with them to any branch they later serve or found.

Local stewardship

The Collegium levies no enrolment, examination, assessment or award fee. Your branch may freely keep local altar offerings, retreat hospitality contributions and study-circle dues to sustain its gatherings. None of these may be required for, or linked to, admission, assessment, rank or diploma.

Your own legal footing

Incorporating is optional, and your Warrant stands whether or not you do. A branch that registers a civil entity or leases premises does so at its own legal, tax and financial responsibility. No branch, Warden or Chaplain may bind the Provost or the Collegium to any debt, contract, lease or liability.

The Feast of Holy Sophia, 30 September

The Collegium’s feast of Wisdom, kept on 30 September (17 September, Julian) in memory of the holy martyrs Faith, Hope and Love and their mother Sophia. Branches may keep it by gathering or in solitary meditation, with the lighting of a candle and the Order of Prayer. Where regalia is worn, it is a solemn occasion for gown and hood. The Warrant of Constitution

From three people to a chartered branch

The Warrant is granted gladly wherever the Foundational Statutes are honoured. Here is the whole journey.

  1. 1

    Three or more Collegians petition

    Three or more Collegians in good standing write to the Provost. Name your first Warden, and a Chaplain if you are an Oratory. The Secretary-General receives and prepares your petition.

  2. 2

    The Provost grants the Warrant

    The Warrant is granted where the Statutes are honoured, and your first Warden is confirmed by the grant. Your name, insignia and marks are used by licence of the Warrant.

  3. 3

    The Chancery equips you

    You receive the Model Constitution, your website listing, regalia guidance and the Warden’s medallion. Adopt your own Constitution and begin to gather.

  4. 4

    Keep a short Annual Return

    File a brief return with the Chancery at least once every fifteen months. If one is overdue after a written reminder, the Warrant is marked Dormant, and it lapses after twelve months of dormancy. This touches the branch only, never anyone’s rank or good standing.

  5. 5

    Keep at least three at your gatherings

    The Warden counts. If you fall below three you have three months to recover. If the Warrant lapses it is without prejudice: everyone keeps their awards and good standing, and you may petition again when three gather.

  6. 6

    Fraternal counsel before anything else

    In the case of a serious departure from collegiate order, the Provost first offers fraternal counsel, then written notice and a fair chance to respond. Only where urgent harm requires may a Warrant be suspended at once.

Care comes first

Safeguarding & pastoral care

Every Chapter and Oratory adopts the safeguarding provisions of the Model Constitution. A branch is a place where people are safe, and where no one is pressed to choose prayer over proper care.

  • No one is left alone in ministry with a child or an adult at risk, and concerns are reported to the Chancery without delay.
  • Nothing in a branch’s life may discourage anyone from seeking medical, psychological or other professional help. Prayer, meditation and healing prayer are spiritual practices, not treatment.
  • A concern about anyone, including an officer of the Collegium, may also go to the civil authorities, and any legal duty to report in your place is never overridden.
  • No rite or practice that harms, demeans or coerces anyone may be used by any branch.
Plain answers

Questions people ask

Do we have to be ordained?

No. A Warden may be lay or ordained. The Warden must be a Collegian in good standing holding the level of Associate, Licentiate or Fellow. Only a Chaplain must be an ordained Priest or Prelate, and a Chaplain is optional.

We are not Collegians yet. Can we still start a branch?

The petition must come from three or more Collegians in good standing, so each petitioner first needs a level of the Collegium (Associate or above). Enrolment and assessment are free, and the Associate level is the foundation. See the Collegiate Roll for the routes.

Can our branch ask for offerings or dues?

Locally, yes: altar offerings, retreat hospitality contributions and study-circle dues are yours to keep. But none may be required for, or linked to, admission, assessment, any award or diploma, and no award-related fee may ever be charged in the Collegium’s name.

Which rite does an Oratory use?

An Oratory uses the ACOA Rite, the Authorised Eucharistic Liturgy of the Australian Church of Antioch, so far as the ACOA permits its use by the Collegium, and any other rite the Provost approves in writing and records in the Register of Approved Rites. The Collegium does not ordain, license or rule on the validity of any celebrant’s orders.

Do we have to incorporate?

No. The Collegium supports incorporation for good governance, but it is optional, and your Warrant stands either way.

Can a branch grant awards or diplomas?

No. Awards and diplomas are granted only by the central Chancery under the Great Seal. Your branch can, however, welcome and support those who study toward them.

What if our numbers fall?

You have three months to recover to three participants. If the Warrant lapses, it is without prejudice. Nobody loses an award or standing, and you may petition again whenever three gather.

Begin where you are

Ready to gather?

Write to the Chancery with the names of at least three Collegians in good standing, the kind of branch you wish to form, your proposed Warden, and a Chaplain if you intend an Oratory. The Provost grants the Warrant gladly.
