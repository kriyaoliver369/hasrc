Non-Stipendiary Learned Society • Free Enrolment

The HASRC Fellowship Framework

An ecclesiastical learned society with three assessed levels, recognising progressive scholarly attainment, ministerial competence, and institutional stewardship within the Antiochian charism. Every level is earned by assessment. (Our Statutes call these levels ranks.)

Freely offered: The Collegium charges no membership dues, and no fee for enrolment, examination, assessment, candidacy or any award, before or after a successful assessment. The work of the Collegium is sustained by voluntary freewill offerings, which are never a condition of any award. The absence of fees relaxes no academic standard.
Level 1 • Foundational

Associate of the Collegium

AHASRC

A foundational scholar: one who has completed Modules 1 to 3 of a specialist stream and a Synthesis Theological Paper, or who shows equal standing by clergy service in early orders or by a recognised Bachelor’s degree.

Routes (fulfil one):

  • Route A, curricular examination: Modules 1–3 and a Synthesis Theological Paper, within a 6-month self-paced window.
  • Route B, clergy & portfolio: a Subdeacon or Deacon with 2 years’ service, or a Priest with 1–4 years’ ministry, with a portfolio dossier and a 2,000-word paper.
  • Route C, academic degree bridge: a recognised Bachelor’s degree and a Theological Paper.

Rights & privileges:

  • The post-nominal AHASRC, with a stream designation for Route A.
  • The right to submit research, reviews and essays to JOIS for peer review.
  • Participation in closed collegiate seminars and symposiums.
  • Route A Associates carry credit for Modules 1–3 toward the Licentiate.

Level 2 • Specialist

Licentiate of the Collegium

LHASRC

A specialist scholar licensed to teach (licentia docendi) in one discipline: one who has completed the six modules and the Capstone Treatise of a specialist stream, or who shows equal standing by at least five years of ministry or by a recognised Master’s degree.

Routes (fulfil one):

  • Route A, full curricular examination: six modules and an 8,000-word Capstone Treatise, within a 12-month window.
  • Route B, ministerial & RPEL: a Permanent Deacon, Priest or Bishop with at least 5 years of ministry, a portfolio dossier and the Capstone Treatise.
  • Route C, graduate degree bridge: a Master’s degree, with advanced standing of up to three modules.

Rights & privileges:

  • The post-nominal LHASRC, alone or with the discipline style (L.S.Min, L.Mphy.Th., L.AMsc., L.C.Sp.).
  • Priority consideration for monograph publication in JOIS.
  • The prerogative to sponsor and recommend candidates for the Associate rank.
  • Eligibility to petition partner institutions for advanced standing, where an articulation agreement is in force.

Crimson-lined hood • 1-inch gold satin binding

Level 3 • Highest substantive

Fellow of the Collegium

FHASRC

A senior scholar of research, at senior research level (rigour comparable to a Master of Arts thesis): one who has published research in JOIS or defended a Research Monograph, or who holds a Doctorate, or who is a bishop holding the Licentiate on the Episcopal Bench. Consecration alone does not confer Fellowship.

Routes (fulfil one):

  • Route A, JOIS research & monograph: three JOIS articles, or an 18,000–25,000-word Research Monograph, with the Licentiate or a Master’s degree.
  • Route B, doctoral & published author: a Doctorate with JOIS articles, or qualifying published books or indexed articles.
  • Route C, Episcopal Bench: a bishop with the Licentiate, and either 3 years’ collegiate tenure or two JOIS articles.
  • Route D, discipline Fellowship: a defended Research Monograph in one stream, within 12 months of petition, for a discipline designation.

Rights & privileges:

  • The post-nominal FHASRC, with a discipline designation for Route D.
  • A permanent seat on the Academic Senate, with Consultative Voice.
  • The prerogative to sponsor and endorse candidates for the Associate and Licentiate ranks.
  • Eligibility to serve as a JOIS peer reviewer, at the invitation of the Editor-in-Chief.

The Associate rank does not lead automatically to Fellowship. A candidate must first earn the Licentiate, hold an earned graduate or doctoral degree, or qualify as a published author under Fellowship Route B. Recognition of prior learning (RPEL) applies only to the Associate and Licentiate routes that name it, never to the Fellowship. The Founder’s Statutes and Ordinances govern every route.

Discipline Designations

The stream in which a level was earned by examination is named alongside it. Associate designations apply to Route A only, and Fellow designations to Route D only.

Honours Outside the Ladder

Conferred by Decree of the Provost, always styled honoris causa, and carrying no examination-based standing. They are not levels, and no one reaches them by progression.

HonFHASRC

Honorary Fellow

Presiding Archbishops, Primates, External Ordinaries and distinguished scholars whose ministry or patronage elevates the Collegium. No seat on the Academic Senate.

FHASRC (Em.)

Fellow Emeritus

Venerable retired prelates, founding figures and senior spiritual pillars of the tradition, honoured in perpetual thanksgiving.

FHASRC (Founding)

Founding Fellow

Held by the Founder, The Most Reverend Mar Elisha Thoma, by the Statutes. Not an examined rank, and kept for life.

MHASRC • Reserved

Member of the Collegium

A general affiliative tier, not yet active. If the Provost opens it, it will carry no subscription or tithe.

Public Register of Awards

The Chancery records each numbered certificate, its level, post-nominal and date of award, so that anyone can verify an award by certificate number. Holders may ask for personal details to be withheld.

Perpetual & in Good Standing

Awards are permanent. They are withdrawn only for falsification or grave canonical delinquency, after written notice. Good standing never depends on payment, attendance or activity.

Apply for a Level

Choose the level and the route that fit your attainment, then write to the Chancery with the documents that route requires: certified copies of degrees or letters of orders, a ministerial curriculum vitae, or your paper or treatise. At least two assessors read every submission. The Chancery aims to decide within ninety days of a complete submission, though this creates no entitlement to any outcome.

There is no fee at any stage.
