    Straight answers

    Is it really free?

    Yes. And because free makes sensible people suspicious, here is exactly why, who pays, and what you are not signing up to.

    Yes, and it is in the Statutes

    Enrolment, examination, assessment, candidacy, the award itself and the Register cost nothing. That promise is written into the Collegium’s Statutes (§10.5) and overrides every other clause. No award can be bought, and none is made on request or on an offering alone.

    Free, but not free of effort

    No fee does not mean no homework. Expect modules to read, essays to write, and an 8,000-word treatise at the Licentiate level. Two readers assess everything you submit, and nothing is awarded for turning up. You pay with your attention, not your wallet.

    Why free: a ministry open to all

    The Collegium is a sacramental ministry and learned society, not a business. It is not-for-profit and pays no dividends. If it ever closed, its assets could not be shared out among members but would pass to a kindred jurisdiction, library or trust (§10.1, §10.4). Its altar is open to all, and its Statutes bar discrimination in admission and assessment (§5.7). Free is not a marketing offer. It is how a ministry that is open to all has to work.

    Who pays, and who benefits

    The Collegium is sustained by voluntary freewill offerings, and by grants and bequests from aligned bodies and generous individuals. They pay for the servers and archives, the Chancery’s administration, the journal, and the preparation of diplomas (§10.2). That means:

    • A student with no spare money studies on the same terms as anyone else.
    • The journal, JOIS, is free to read and free to publish in, with no author fees.
    • A living tradition is preserved and taught for the next generation, and local chapters and oratories can gather around it.

    Officers serve without stipend. The Statutes allow only contracted Chancery staff and a modest honorarium for the Provost, paid only from a year’s surplus after a twelve-month reserve and confirmed by an Examiner of Accounts. They also require a yearly statement of offerings received and how they were used (§10.5(6), §10.7).

    Not a trap: no church to join

    Studying here does not enlist you in a church. The Collegium is a scholarly and contemplative society, not a parish or diocese (§1.2). There are no loyalty oaths and no test of belief: the acknowledgement signed before a diploma is issued is an undertaking of good order, not a creed (§5.7, §6.6). Admission never depends on your denomination or background. You may withdraw by written notice, and an award already made stays valid (§5.6, §6.8).

    Not an upsell

    No price, suggested amount or payment prompt appears anywhere in enrolment, study or assessment (§10.5). Offerings go through a separate channel, and assessors are never told whether a candidate has given one. If books, courses or other resources are offered in future, they will stay separate from the path to an award, and will never be required for it or linked to it.

    More questions

    How long does it take?

    It is self-paced, with no minimum time. Candidacy runs for at most six months at the Associate level and twelve months at the Licentiate level and for Fellowship by Route D. You can submit whenever your work is ready, and the Chancery aims to decide within ninety days of a complete submission.

    What is an award worth, and what is it not?

    It is an ecclesiastical award, recognised within the Collegium, its affiliated bodies and the wider Independent Sacramental Movement. It is not a civilly accredited degree, not a professional qualification, and not ordination: completing a programme prepares you, but ordination remains a separate act of your own ordaining bishop (§6.3.1).

    I am not Christian, or I belong to another church. May I study?

    Yes. Admission is open to qualified seekers, scholars, clergy and practitioners of every background (§5.7). The Collegium itself is Christian and sacramental, and its five pillars are the tradition’s own teaching, but they do not bind your inquiry or your conclusions (§2.2).

    Who do I write to?

    The Chancery, at info@hasrcollegium.com. The enrolment form above opens an email with your choices ready.

    Associate, Licentiate & Fellow

    The Collegium recognises progressive levels of scholarly attainment, ministerial competence, and institutional stewardship. Each level is awarded only on assessed work, by one of the routes set out in Ordinance 5. Enrolment, examination, assessment and award are free of charge under every route, and no level is a certificate of personal spiritual attainment. (Our Statutes call these levels ranks.)

    The Five Pillars of the Spruitian Charism

    Erected by Archiepiscopal Decree as an autonomous ecclesiastical learned society, HASRC studies spirituality as scientia, ordered knowledge joined to wisdom, through five codified pillars:

    • 1. Metaphysical-Sacramental Synthesis: The harmonious fusion of traditional Catholic sacramental realism (the seven historic sacraments as conduits of grace and subtle energy) with New Thought, Metaphysical Science, spiritual mind healing, and creative consciousness.
    • 2. Christ-Centred Universalism & Meta-Catholicity: Grounding theological inquiry in the living, personal, and cosmic Christ, wherein unconditional Love is revered as the supreme spiritual reality, transcending rigid dogmatism and sectarian exclusivity.
    • 3. Sacramental Justice & Radical Inclusion: Upholding the Open Altar, welcoming all seekers to the Eucharist with dignity regardless of background, and affirming universal equality in Holy Orders across unbroken female and male apostolic lineages.
    • 4. Apostolic Convergence: Honouring the gathering and preservation of 19 historic streams of Apostolic Succession not as bureaucratic legalism, but as a mystical sacrament of Christian unity and spiritual empowerment.
    • 5. Spiritual Engineering & Altar Energetics: Bridging theoretical scholarship with practical spiritual progression through contemplative protocols, liturgical metaphysics, and transformative inner technologies.

    Best Practice I

    Spiritual & Charism Fidelity

    Grounding the Collegium’s research and training in the authentic inner-Christian and metaphysical charism of our spiritual predecessors. Candidate research engaging the Spruitian Charism identifies at least one of the foundational pillars, examining it with evidence, primary texts, and rigorous critical argument. Assessors evaluate the depth of scholarship rather than requiring ideological uniformity.

    Best Practice II

    Open & Ethical Methodology

    Maintaining transparency of primary sources, intellectual honesty, rigorous APA 7th Edition citation, and absolute respect for the free will, personal privacy, and well-being of every participant. All submissions undergo assessment by non-conflicted scholarly assessors.
