1911–1994 • Founding Patriarch[cite: 51, 72]

His Sacred Beatitude, Patriarch Herman Adrian Spruit

First Hierach of the Catholic Apostolic Church of Antioch & a prominent Grandfather of the Independent Sacramental Movement

[cite: 51, 72]

Consecration: 1957 (Hampton, Wadle, & Marshall)
Jurisdiction: Catholic Apostolic Church of Antioch (1959)[cite: 51, 72]
Charism: Meta-Catholic love, open communion, sacramental justice[cite: 51, 72]
Custodianship: 17 consolidated lines (Roman-Old Catholic, Syrian-Antiochene, Armenian, Syro-Chaldean, OCR, Anglican, Polish, Greek, Constantinople, Russion, Welsh, Non-Juring/Irish, Culdee, Gallican, Chaldean-Uniate, Coptic, Albanian)

Born in the Netherlands with early exposure to the Old Catholic tradition before his family embraced Methodism, Patriarch Spruit emigrated to the United States in 1927. After fruitful years in Methodist ministry in California, his spiritual quest led him toward deeper experiential realms, serving in leadership within Ernest Holmes' Religious Science movement where he mastered principles of spiritual mind healing and consciousness.

Through Charles Leadbeater's The Science of the Sacraments and encounters with Bishop John Hampton and Archbishop Lowell Wadle, he re-discovered the sacramental altar. Consecrated to the episcopate in 1957 in lineages tracing to Archbishop Joseph René Vilatte, he begin the ministry of the Catholic Apostolic Church of Antioch in 1959. Patriarch Spruit consolidated 17 distinct historic apostolic lineages, viewing multiple consecrations sub conditione as an operative act of embodying Christian unit.

He pioneered "sacramental justice"—offering open communion without dogma and courageously extending holy orders to women, including the 1974 consecration of Jennie Maiereder as one of the first female Catholic bishops in the modern independent movement. In sacred partnership with Matriarch +++Meri Spruit, he established a co-equal leadership model balancing masculine and feminine spiritual principles. In 1987, during their historic visit to Australia, Patriarch Herman and Matriarch Meri received the ancient Druidic and Celtic lines of succession from Archdruid Cluthin & Archmage Ryan, and further entrusting these specific lines to Archbishops Frank and Chearle Bugge, adding to them 19 lines in total.

Primate Emeritus • Over 40 Years of Service[cite: 51, 72]

His Beatitude, Patriarch Frank Bugge, DD

First Hierarch of The Australian Church of Antioch (ACoA) & Primate Emeritus

Consecration: 1987 (By Patriarch Herman & Matriarch Meri Spruit)[cite: 51, 72]
Office: First Hierarch of ACoA; Primate Emeritus
Mastery: Theology, Numerology, Metaphysical Counseling, Esoteric Master
Academic Legacy: Principal textbook author for SMDS seminary training over 4 decades[cite: 51, 72]

Archbishop Frank Bugge's spiritual life is marked by profound synthesis, bridging practical secular mastery as an electrician, motor mechanic, engineer, and truck driver with innate clairvoyant sensitivity from childhood. Drawn to church liturgy as a teenage organist, he continuously sought deeper spiritual realities beyond rigid exoteric dogma.

In the early 1970s, he connected with Rev. Father John King of the Liberal Catholic Church, who introduced him to pure numerology. Over the next three decades, expanded through advanced tutelage under Druid Archmage Ryan, Archbishop Bugge refined this esoteric system into a transformative pastoral counseling tool. His path culminated in extensive, intimate study directly under Patriarch Herman Adrian Spruit and Matriarch Meri Spruit.

In 1987, Archbishop Frank and Archbishop Chearle Bugge were consecrated by Patriarch Herman and Matriarch Meri, officially establishing the Australian jurisdiction with the 17 Antiochian apostolic lines, alongside the distinct Celtic and Druidic Christian lines. As longtime Presiding Archbishop and Primate Emeritus, he authored numerous foundational textbooks for St. Michael's Divinity School (SMDS), anchoring the Australian church in radical hospitality: refusing to judge any soul by marital status, orientation, or past life circumstances.

Primate • Over 30-years of Ministry

His Eminence, The Most Reverend Glenn W. Taylor, DD[cite: 51, 72]

Presiding Archbishop and Primate of The Australian Church of Antioch (ACoA)

DD, F. Dip FSSB, FCMD, LCMD, L Dip A (Aust), ANZM, ANZT, Hon SCSM, EOSF[cite: 51, 72]

Ecclesiastical Offices[cite: 51, 72]

Primacy: Primate of ACoA (Since 2025)

Seminary: Dean, St. Michael's Divinity School (SMDS)[cite: 51, 72]
Distinction: Clergy Formation, Academic Development, Theologian, Franciscan Scholar, Musician and Music Examiner

Archbishop Glenn W. Taylor's leadership embodies a harmonious synthesis of three distinct threads: unwavering pastoral ministry, rigorous theological scholarship, and profound musical excellence. As the founding Minister General of the Ecumenical Order of St. Francis (TOR), known in religion as Brother Paul Francis (eosf), his ministry unites contemplative Franciscan simplicity with contemporary apostolic order.

His ecclesiastical journey began in 1972 with over three decades of foundational service in the Anglican Church (St. John's Launceston and St. George's), serving on the Parish Council, Diocesan Education Committee, teaching the intensive 40-week Bethel Bible Overview, and directing youth missions. In 2002, he transferred his Franciscan vows to Primate Frank Bugge. Ordained Deacon and Priest in September 2002, he was elected to the Episcopate in 2004 as Bishop of Tasmania, elevated to Archbishop of Tasmania in 2006, and formally installed as Presiding Archbishop and Primate of the Australian Church of Antioch on February 16, 2025.

Archbishop Taylor has long served as Dean of St. Michael's Divinity School (SMDS), on the Board of Studies for St. Bonaventure College of Franciscan Studies, and as Honorary Vice-Chancellor of the Antioch School of Ministry. Concurrently, he is an accomplished master musician proficient across ten instruments, holding a Licentiate in Viola Teacher Diploma, Fellowship with the Council of Music and Drama, author of the ANZCA string syllabus, and veteran national examiner.

1951–2024 • In Memoriam[cite: 51, 72]

Her Grace, The Most Reverend Leonie Newman[cite: 51, 72]

Dip. Th, LSMDS • Primate of The Temple of Ancient Wisdom (TAW)

Archival Summary[cite: 51, 72]

Consecration: 2014; Elevated to Archbishop 2019
Key Role: Primate, The Temple of Ancient Wisdom (TAW)
Offices: Secretary to Synod; Assistant to Dean of SMDS
Ministry: Pastoral counseling, women ministry, healing, esoteric documentation

Her Grace, Archbishop Leonie Newman (July 30, 1951 – October 1, 2024) was a prominent Australian spiritual leader within the Australian Church of Antioch whose scholarly devotion to primordial esoteric spirituality left an indelible mark on independent sacramental communities across Australia and internationally.

Her steady progression in holy orders commenced with her ordination to the Priesthood in March 2009, advancement to Archpriest in July 2013, consecration to the Episcopate on September 28, 2014, and ultimate elevation to Archbishop on January 20, 2019. Throughout her tenure, she held significant synodical responsibilities, including Archbishop for the S.E. Peninsula Region, Secretary to the Synod, Assistant to the Dean of SMDS, Webmaster for ACoA, Head of the Parish/Order of St. Bernadette of Antioch in Bonbeach, Victoria, and Registered Marriage Celebrant.

Central to her enduring legacy was her leadership as Primate of The Temple of Ancient Wisdom (TAW), an Order functioning under the protectorate and constitution of the ACoA. Under her direction, the Temple documented and disseminated ancient teachings not previously in the public domain. Her pastoral ministry specialized in spiritual counseling based on birthdates and numbers, energetic spiritual healings, the traditional rite of exorcism, and meticulous archival documentation for the church. Being a fervent student of the Patriarch +++Frank Bugge and Druid Archmage Ryan, she is instrumental in supporting and guiding the HASRC formation in its initial stages.

Best Practice I

Spiritual & Charism Fidelity

We ensure all research, training programs, and spiritual methods are authentically grounded in the specific 'inner Christian' charism of our lineage. This unconventional, practical approach focuses on spiritual engineering—developing the specific rites, meditations, and metaphysical techniques passed down and evolved from our spiritual predecessors, distinguishing our work from more common exoteric traditions.

Best Practice II

Open & Ethical Methodology

We employ rigorous, intellectually honest methods in our research and are transparent about our sources. In our training programs, we adhere to the highest ethical standards, ensuring all spiritual methods are taught with clarity, integrity, and profound respect for the well-being and free will of every participant.

Best Practice III

Practical & Contextual Responsibility

We bridge scholarly research with tangible spiritual progression. Our "charism" lies in developing practical applications—such as spiritual methods, meditation protocols, and rites—derived directly from our research. We are responsible for teaching these practices within their proper spiritual context, ensuring participants understand their purpose, application, and scope as tools for personal development.

Best Practice IV

Inclusive & Respectful Dialogue

We foster an inclusive hub that welcomes scholars, practitioners, and seekers from all backgrounds who share a serious interest in our work. We engage in respectful dialogue with other spiritual, scientific, and academic traditions, positioning our unique research and training methods as a valuable contribution to the broader conversation on inner spirituality and human development.
