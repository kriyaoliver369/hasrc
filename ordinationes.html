About this edition

What follows is the text of the Ordinationes itself, word for word, with its own numbering. Nothing has been reworded. Ordinances that concern the Collegium’s inward order among its officers are not reproduced, and each place is marked in the text. The Ordinances on ranks, examination, branches and regalia, and the paths from petition to award, are given in full.

The Ordinances are subordinate to the Statuta Fundamentalia (Section 11.5). Where this edition and the full Ordinationes differ, the full Ordinationes govern, and the Statutes govern both.

Promulgation of the Ordinances

These Ordinances are made by Decree of the Provost under Section 11.5 of the Statuta Fundamentalia. They are subordinate to the Statutes and have force only so far as they are consistent with them. In these Ordinances “Section” means a Section of the Statuta Fundamentalia, and “Ordinance” means an Ordinance in this text. Words with capital initials have the meanings given in Section 11.4 of the Statutes. A change to a requirement for a rank applies to petitions filed after it takes effect (Section 11.5(3)), and no change reduces a rank already conferred. The Formularium (Book of Forms), named in the Schedule of Instruments of the Statutes, carries the forms that Ordinances 12 to 14 call for. The Tables at the end of these Ordinances are a guide to the text, and the text governs.
Part A

The Branches

Ordinance 1Wardens, Chaplains and the Life of a Branch

Made under Section 1.4. The branches themselves, their Warrants and their reserved matters are provided for in the Statutes.
1. Every branch, whether a Chapter or an Oratory, is presided over by a Warden (lay or ordained) as the presiding officer and guardian of that Chapter or Oratory: convening its gatherings, upholding its Constitution and the Foundational Statutes, and representing it in communion with the Chancery. A Warden must be a Collegian in good standing who holds a rank of AHASRC, LHASRC or FHASRC, as Section 1.4(1) requires. The first Warden is named in the petition for the Warrant, and may be one of the petitioners, and is confirmed by the grant of the Warrant. Later Wardens are elected by the members and confirmed by the Provost, unless the person elected lacks the required qualification.
2. The Provost may remove a Warden or Chaplain only for cause under the Model Constitution, and may not impose one the members have not chosen. An Oratory may also have a Chaplain, an ordained Priest or Prelate who is a Collegian in good standing, named in the petition or later chosen by its members and confirmed by the Provost as for a Warden, for its sacramental and pastoral work.
3. Support from the Chancery. Every chartered branch receives the Model Constitution, listing on the Collegium’s website, guidance on regalia, the medallion of office of its Warden (and Chaplain, if any), and access to curricular materials and visiting speakers.
4. Annual Return and Dormancy. Each branch files a short Annual Return with the Chancery at least once in every fifteen months. If a return is overdue after written reminder, the Warrant is marked Dormant, and a branch dormant for twelve months lapses. This concerns the branch only, and never affects the rank or good standing of any Collegian.
5. Minimum Strength and Lapse. A branch keeps at least three (3) participants at its gatherings, the Warden being counted. If it falls below three, it has three (3) months to recover. If it does not recover, the Warrant lapses without prejudice. A lapse is not a revocation under Section 1.4(7): the Warden and every member keep their rank and good standing, and the branch may petition again when three (3) gather.
6. Senior Warden and Senior Chaplain. After five (5) years of cumulative active service as a Warden, or as a Chaplain, in good standing, the Provost recognises the holder as Senior Warden or Senior Chaplain. This is an honour and not a rank. It carries no authority and no precedence over any other Warden or Chaplain, save a place on the Roll of Voters under Section 11.6(4). Service counts only while the holder is active, and the count returns to zero if the holder leads no gathering for three (3) consecutive months, unless the Provost has granted leave, during which the count is paused. A Warden or Chaplain who resigns, or whose Warrant lapses, before completing the five years keeps no title and no credit. Once conferred, the honour is held for life, including after the holder gains a higher rank, and the one-band epitoge is kept for life as the symbol of long ministry, under Ordinance 11.4. The title belongs to the holder and not to any branch. It is kept for life, whether the holder retires, takes leave or moves, and it continues in any branch the holder later serves or founds, in any place. The honour is withdrawn only for grave canonical delinquency under Section 11.4.
Part B

The Bodies of the Collegium

Ordinance 2The Academic Senate (Senatus Academicus)

Made under Section 4.4. The Senate is advisory only (Section 4.3(2)) and has the tasks below and no others (Section 4.8).
1. Prerogatives & Duties: Seated Fellows of the Academic Senate exercise their Consultative Voice and collegiate standing through the following duties and privileges:
Editorial Peer Review: Serving as collegiate assessors in the peer-review process of the Journal of Inner Spirituality (JOIS) and official monographs at the sole invitation and discretion of the Editor-in-Chief, as determined by relevant subject-matter expertise, reviewer availability, and the absence of conflict of interest. A seat in the Academic Senate, held automatically by every substantive Fellow upon admission (Section 4.4(2)), confers eligibility to be called upon for peer review, but constitutes neither an obligation on the journal to assign manuscripts nor an entitlement to review every submission.
Academic Assessment: Reading submitted portfolios, prior-learning dossiers, and capstone treatises of candidates petitioning for the Associate (AHASRC), Licentiate (LHASRC), or Fellowship (FHASRC) ranks when assigned by the Dean of Studies, and sitting on Review Panels under item 2.
Candidate Sponsorship: Holding the formal prerogative to sponsor, nominate, and endorse qualified candidates for admission or advancement within the Collegium.
Research & Archival Advisement: Proposing thematic research streams, liturgical commentaries, manuscript translations, and archival preservation initiatives.
Charismatic Fidelity & Discretion: Upholding the WRITES core values and the Spruitian Charism in public scholarship, while maintaining strict collegiate confidentiality regarding candidate evaluations and internal Senate deliberations.
2. Review Panels: The Senate acts on a petition through a Review Panel and not in full session. For every petition for the Fellowship, by whatever route, the Panel is three persons. The Dean of Studies sits as the first member and assigns the other two, each of whom is a Fellow, an officer of the Collegium or an invited reader. While the office of Dean of Studies is united in the Provost under Section 4.2, the Provost sits as Dean of Studies and assigns the other two in the same way. Once a Dean of Studies is appointed, the Provost does not sit on a Panel. An invited reader is a prelate, or a scholar who holds an earned doctorate, is asked for that petition only, and holds no seat in the Senate. The Dean of Studies (or the Provost sitting as Dean) and the second member are the two assessors under Section 6.5(2). The Panel reads the work, may put questions to the candidate in writing or by video, and gives its recommendation in a written report with reasons: to recommend, to recommend after stated corrections, or not to recommend. The Panel decides by majority and records any dissent. A person with a personal interest in the petition, or who sponsored the candidate, does not sit on that Panel. The Panel recommends, and the Provost confers the rank by decree under the Great Seal. The recommendation is advice. A rank is conferred only by decree of the Provost under Section 4.3, or by the vote of the Elder Council under Section 4.7(4).
3. Sessions: The Senate meets, in writing or by video, when the Provost convenes it on a matter of scholarship, on the Provost’s own motion or at the request of the Dean of Studies. In a regency the Acting Provost convenes it in the Provost’s name (Section 11.3(7)(c)). The Senate gives its advice by a majority of those who answer. The advice is recorded with any dissent and is advice only, as Section 4.3(2) provides. The Secretary-General, or a Fellow named by the Provost, keeps the minutes.
4. Service and Withdrawal: Membership of the Senate is a service. A Fellow may withdraw from it at any time by written notice, and may take the seat again on written notice, without loss of the Fellowship, the rank, the post-nominal or the regalia, which are kept under Section 5.6.
6. Vacancy in the Offices of Section 4.2: The Provost may appoint, in advance and by formal decree under Section 4.2, a deputy Dean of Studies, a deputy Keeper of the Archives and a deputy Editor-in-Chief. If no deputy is in office and the Provost cannot be reached, assessment, Review Panels and editorial screening wait until the Provost or an Acting Provost can act, and the service aims in Sections 6.5(3) and 6.8(2) are suspended meanwhile. In a regency the Acting Provost performs these offices as part of the Provost’s powers under Section 11.3(7)(c).

Ordinance 3The Secretary-General (Secretarius Generalis)

Made under Section 4.6.
1. Standing Delegation: The Secretary-General acts in the Provost’s name, for the good governance of the Collegium, in the routine administration of the Collegium and in such matters as the Provost lists in a written Schedule of Delegations. These include appointing Legates under Section 6.9, granting leave to Wardens and Chaplains, receiving Annual Returns, and receiving and preparing Warrant petitions. The Schedule may also list the conferral of a rank on a candidate who has completed every assessment, recommendation and requirement of Articles V and VI. The Secretary-General then confers the rank by decree in the Provost’s name, signing with the Custodian as Section 6.6(5)(e) provides, and no meeting of the Elder Council is needed unless the Schedule says so. The Secretary-General may decide these matters in the Provost’s absence. The Secretary-General may suspend a Warrant where urgent harm requires under Section 1.4(7), and may revoke a Warrant only with the Provost’s written consent, after fraternal counsel, at least three (3) recorded consultations with the affected branch, written notice and a fair chance to respond, and subject to review under Section 6.8. If the Provost cannot be reached, the Secretary-General may suspend but may not revoke, except on a vote of the Elder Council under Section 4.7.
2. Review: A decision of the Secretary-General under Article VI is open to review under Section 6.8 like any other decision of the Chancery.

Part C

Ranks, Studies and Examination

Ordinance 5Requirements for the Ranks

Made under Section 5.4. Each rank is conferred only on assessed attainment, by one of the routes below. The meaning of each rank is set out in Section 5.2, and its permanence in Section 5.6.
1. Associate of the Collegium (AHASRC)
Pathways to Admission (Fulfilment of ONE route required):
Route A (Curricular Examination Track):
Academic Requirement: Enrolment in and completion of Modules 1, 2, and 3 of the chosen specialist stream, being the fixed foundational sequence of that stream set out in Section 6.3, within a 6-month self-paced examination window.
Deliverable: Submission of a single Consolidated Examination Folio containing three (3) Module Essays (2,000 words each) plus one (1) Synthesis Theological Paper (2,000 words) in APA 7th Edition style (8,000 words total).
Route B (Clergy & RPEL Portfolio Track):
Eligibility: Open to clergy serving in apostolic or independent sacramental traditions holding EITHER:
(a) Minor Orders & Diaconate: Ordination as a Subdeacon or Deacon with a minimum of 2 years of active or cumulative altar/parish service; OR
(b) Presbyterate (Early Priesthood): Ordination as a Priest with 1 to 4 years of active pastoral or sacramental ministry.
Deliverable: Submission of a Ministerial Portfolio Dossier containing:
(a) Certified copies of Letters of Holy Orders and verified Lineage Documentation.
(b) A comprehensive Ministerial Curriculum Vitae documenting verifiable liturgical and pastoral service.
(c) An original 2,000-word Theological Paper (APA 7th Edition style) on an approved topic mapped to the Associate modules, demonstrating critical engagement with the Spruitian Charism.
Route C (Academic Degree Bridge):
Stream 1 (Accredited Bachelor’s Degree):
(a) Prerequisite: An earned, verified Bachelor’s degree (B.A., B.Sc., B.Th., or higher) from a recognised, accredited university or seminary.
(b) Deliverable: Official transcripts, certificate copy, and an original 2,000-word Theological Paper (APA 7th Edition style) on an approved topic mapped to the Associate modules, bridging the candidate’s discipline with the Spruitian Charism and Collegium research streams.
Stream 2 (Independent & Non-Accredited Seminary Bachelor’s Degree):
(a) Prerequisite: An earned Bachelor’s degree from an independent, autocephalous, or unaccredited ecclesiastical institute or seminary, subject to transcript audit by the Dean of Studies.
(b) Deliverable: Official transcripts, certificate copy, course syllabi, and an expanded 3,500-word Theological Paper (APA 7th Edition style) on an approved topic mapped to the Associate modules, demonstrating formal research methodology and deep engagement with the Spruitian Charism.
2. Licentiate of the Collegium (LHASRC)
Pathways to Admission (Fulfilment of ONE route required):
Route A (Full Curricular Examination Track):
Academic Requirement: Free enrolment and self-paced completion of the six (6) Core Subject Modules and Capstone Treatise of the chosen Licentiate stream within a 12-month candidacy window (with a canonical progress reminder issued at the 9th month). Associates of the same stream admitted under Route A complete Modules 4, 5, and 6 and the Capstone Treatise. An Associate admitted under Route B or Route C takes no modular credit toward the Licentiate by reason of the Associate rank (Section 6.3(4)).
Deliverable: Submission of a single Consolidated Examination Folio containing six (6) dedicated Subject Essays (2,000 words each = 12,000 words) demonstrating mastery of each module textbook, accompanied by one (1) original 8,000-word Capstone Treatise in APA 7th Edition style, structured as Ordinance 9 sets out, synthesising the specialist discipline (20,000 words total).
Route B (Ministerial & RPEL Track):
Eligibility: Open to seasoned clergy serving in apostolic, autocephalous, or independent sacramental traditions (including sacerdotes vagans), being a Permanent Deacon, a Priest or a Bishop.
Deliverable: Submission of an Exhaustive Ministerial Portfolio Dossier comprising:
(a) Certified copies of Letters of Holy Orders (Diaconate, Priesthood or Episcopate) and verified Apostolic Lineage Documentation.
(b) A comprehensive Ministerial Curriculum Vitae documenting a cumulative record of active or intermittent pastoral, liturgical, contemplative, or teaching service spanning a period of at least 5 years.
(c) Verifiable ministerial evidence (sample liturgies, pastoral case studies, retreat programmes, or instructional curricula).
(d) An original 8,000-word Capstone Treatise (APA 7th Edition style) on an approved theological or liturgical topic mapped to the specialist Licentiate discipline, critically engaging the Spruitian Charism.
(e) The Dossier is assessed under Section 5.10 and Ordinance 6 in place of the six (6) Modules, and the candidate proceeds to the Capstone Treatise.
Route C (Graduate Degree Bridge):
Stream 1 (Accredited Master’s Degrees):
(a) Prerequisite: An earned Master’s degree (M.A., M.Sc., M.Div., M.Th., or equivalent) from a recognised, accredited university or seminary.
(b) Advanced Standing: Waiver of three (3) core modules (50% of the modular syllabus), on assessment of the transcripts and documents under Section 5.10 and Ordinance 6.
(c) Deliverable: Official transcripts, certificate copy, and EITHER:
(i) Completion of the remaining three (3) specialist modules (2,000-word essay per module); OR
(ii) Submission of an original 8,000-word Capstone Treatise (APA 7th Edition style) dedicated to Christian mysticism, sacramental theology, liturgical history, or any of the established research streams of the Collegium.
Stream 2 (Independent & Unaccredited Seminary Master’s Degrees):
(a) Prerequisite: An earned Master’s degree from an independent, autocephalous, or unaccredited seminary, subject to transcript audit and curriculum verification by the Dean of Studies.
(b) Advanced Standing: RPEL advanced standing for up to three (3) modules, granted by the Dean of Studies under Section 5.10 and Ordinance 6.
(c) Deliverable: Official transcripts, certificate copy, syllabi of prior graduate study, completion of designated foundational Antiochian reading modules, completion of every module of the stream not given RPEL standing (a 2,000-word essay per module), AND the submission of an original 8,000-word Capstone Treatise (APA 7th Edition style).
3. Fellow of the Collegium (FHASRC)
Pathways to Admission (Fulfilment of ONE route required):
Route A (The JOIS Research & Monograph Track):
Prerequisite: Possession of the Licentiate of the Collegium (LHASRC) or an earned Master’s degree (M.A., M.Sc., M.Div., M.Th.) from a recognised institution.
Scholarly Deliverable (Fulfilment of EITHER option):
(a) Authorship of at least three (3) original, peer-reviewed research articles accepted and published in the Journal of Inner Spirituality (JOIS); OR
(b) Authorship and successful peer evaluation of a comprehensive standalone HASRC Research Monograph (18,000 to 25,000 words in formal academic format) dedicated to Christian mysticism, sacramental theology, liturgical history, or any of the established research streams of the Collegium.
Ratification: Review by a Review Panel under Ordinance 2(2), ratified by Decree of the Provost.
Route B (Doctoral & Published Author Track):
Stream 1 (Accredited Doctorates):
(a) Prerequisite: An earned Doctorate (Ph.D., Th.D., D.D., Ed.D., or secular equivalent) from a recognised, accredited university or seminary.
(b) Deliverable: Doctoral certificate, doctoral abstract, and one (1) original peer-reviewed article published in JOIS. The Dean of Studies verifies the award directly with the awarding institution.
Stream 2 (Independent & Ecclesiastical Doctorates):
(a) Prerequisite: An earned Doctorate from an independent, autocephalous, or unaccredited ecclesiastical seminary, subject to transcript and dissertation verification by the Dean of Studies.
(b) Deliverable: Doctoral certificate, the complete doctoral dissertation (as a PDF or a stable online link), and two (2) original peer-reviewed articles published in JOIS demonstrating rigorous research methodology. The Dean of Studies verifies the award with the awarding seminary, reads the dissertation in full, and checks it for originality. An abstract alone is not accepted from an independent seminary.
Stream 3 (Published Authors: Books and Indexed Articles):
(a) Equivalency: A candidate is credited with two (2) JOIS articles on EITHER:
(i) sole authorship of one (1) substantive, formally published volume bearing an International Standard Book Number (ISBN), issued by an established academic, commercial, or specialist press in the fields of Christian mysticism, sacramental theology, metaphysics, liturgical history, and/or ecclesiology; OR
(ii) authorship of two (2) original peer-reviewed articles in theology, religious studies, or an allied field, published in journals indexed in Scopus at the time of publication.
(b) Only one of these two forms is counted toward the two-article credit. Further works do not reduce the remaining requirement.
(c) Deliverable: The candidate then submits one (1) additional peer-reviewed article to JOIS connecting their work to the Collegium’s research streams.
(d) Verification: Before any credit is given, the Dean of Studies verifies every outside publication: the author’s identity and the DOI or ISBN; that the journal was indexed at the time of publication; that the work is not self-published, predatory, or plagiarised (by originality check); that the candidate is the sole or principal author; and that the subject falls within the Collegium’s established research streams.
Route C (The Episcopal Route):
Prerequisite: Consecration to the Episcopate in documented apostolic succession with verified Letters of Consecration and Lineage, accompanied by the earned Licentiate of the Collegium (LHASRC).
Standing & Assessment (Fulfilment of EITHER benchmark):
(a) Three (3) consecutive years of standing as a Licentiate in good standing, counted from the date of conferral of the Licentiate shown in the Register and completed before the Fellowship petition is filed; OR
(b) Authorship and publication of two (2) original peer-reviewed research articles in the Journal of Inner Spirituality (JOIS).
Route D (Discipline Fellowship by Research Monograph):
Prerequisite: An earned HASRC Licentiate (LHASRC), whether earned in one of the four specialist streams (L.S.Min, L.Mphy.Th., L.AMsc., or L.C.Sp.) or admitted under Route B or Route C.
Scholarly Deliverable: An original, defended Research Monograph of 18,000 to 25,000 words in APA 7th Edition style, structured as Ordinance 9 sets out, at senior research level (rigour comparable to a Master of Arts thesis), within the discipline of the designation sought.
Candidacy: Twelve (12) months from the filing of the Fellowship petition, under Section 6.5.
Ratification: Assessment under Section 6.5, recommendation of a Review Panel under Ordinance 2(2), and ratification by Decree of the Provost.
Designation: Admission as a substantive Fellow (FHASRC), with the discipline designation of the monograph under Section 5.8 (e.g., F.Mphy.Th.).

Ordinance 6The RPEL File

Made under Section 5.10.
1. The File: The candidate presents the RPEL Application Form with a copy of government photographic identification, the documents of holy orders and lineage where the route asks for them, a record of ministry, the evidence in the form’s index, and, for each module claimed, a statement of how the candidate’s learning meets it. The form carries a box in which the candidate gives the consent that Section 9.2(2) asks for.
2. The Decision: The Dean of Studies enters the modules credited by RPEL, and the reasons, in Part 9 of the RPEL Application Form. The decision is open to review under Section 6.8.

Ordinance 7Candidacy Windows

Made under Section 6.5.
1. Maximum Windows, No Minimum: Candidacy runs for a maximum of six (6) months for the Associate, twelve (12) months for the Licentiate (with a progress notice in the 9th month), and twelve (12) months for the Fellowship. The programmes are self-paced: there is no minimum candidacy period, and a candidate may submit whenever the work is complete.
2. Fellowship Window: The 12-month Fellowship window runs from the date the Fellowship petition is filed. The three-year standing benchmark of Route C is an eligibility condition completed before petition and is not counted within the window.
3. Single Extension: One extension may be granted on written petition to the Dean of Studies made before the window closes: up to three (3) months for the Associate, and up to six (6) months for the Licentiate and the Fellowship.
4. Lapse Without Prejudice: A candidacy not submitted by the close of its window, including any extension, lapses without prejudice, and the candidate may re-petition.
5. Admission Windows & Cohorts: The Dean of Studies may open announced admission windows and cap cohort sizes according to assessor capacity.

Ordinance 9Examination Protocol and Academic Apparatus

Made under Section 6.7.
1. Referencing Style: Every folio, paper, treatise, monograph, and portfolio submitted to the Collegium is referenced exclusively in APA 7th Edition style.
2. Nomenclature of Research Deliverables: Each stage culminates in a named written work, and the term dissertation is reserved for the doctoral level:
StageCulminating WorkLength
Associate (AHASRC)Synthesis Theological Paper2,000 words
Licentiate (LHASRC)Capstone Treatise8,000 words
Fellow, Routes A(b) and D (FHASRC)Research Monograph18,000–25,000 words
3. Structure of the Treatise and Monograph: A Capstone Treatise and a Research Monograph follow the customary theological thesis structure: an abstract of up to 300 words; five chapters (Introduction and Research Question; Review of Literature; Method and Sources; Analysis and Discussion; Conclusion); and an APA 7th Edition reference list.
4. Source Density: A Capstone Treatise cites a minimum of twenty-five (25) primary, patristic, archival, or peer-reviewed sources. A Research Monograph is expected to exceed this substantially.
5. Prior Approval of Topic: Before writing, a Capstone Treatise or Research Monograph candidate submits a 300-word proposal and provisional bibliography to the Dean of Studies for approval, within the approved domains of the relevant Study Pack.
6. Word Counts: Stated word counts exclude the title page, abstract, reference list, and appendices.
7. Desk-Reject Rule: Incomplete folios, works falling more than 10% below the required word count, works exceeding a stated maximum, and submissions lacking a formal reference apparatus are returned unassessed for correction. Subjective journaling, devotional piety, pulpit homilies, and unreferenced speculation are excluded from assessment.
8. Folio Assembly: Deliverables are bundled into one PDF folio transmitted to the Chancery, accompanied by a copy of government photo identification (handled under Section 9.2) and an executed Declaration of Academic Integrity in the form set out in the relevant Study Pack.
9. Fresh Work: A Capstone Treatise or Research Monograph is a new work written for the petition, or the substantial completion of earlier research that has never been submitted for any award or published. (a) Disclosure: in the proposal under item 5 the candidate discloses any earlier drafts, the stage reached, and any supervisor or institution connected with them. (b) Not already examined: a thesis, dissertation or paper that has been examined or accepted for any award, or published, elsewhere may not be submitted in whole or in substantial part as the candidate’s Treatise or Monograph. It may be cited as a source. (c) Unfinished work is welcome: a candidate whose research was begun years ago and never completed, such as unfinished doctoral work, may complete it for this purpose if (a) is met and the Dean of Studies is satisfied that not less than one half of the text submitted is written after the petition is filed. (d) Check: every Treatise and Monograph is checked for originality and for undisclosed reuse, and undisclosed reuse is falsification under Section 6.6(4).
10. Engagement with the Spruitian Charism: Where Ordinance 5 asks for engagement with the Spruitian Charism, the work identifies at least one of the five pillars set out in Section 2.2, which are guiding principles, explains it from the Collegium’s own sources, and examines it with evidence and argument. The assessors judge the quality of the engagement and not agreement with it. The pillars do not bind the inquiry or the conclusions of the candidate (Section 2.2 and Section 5.7(3)).

Ordinance 10Legates and the Presentation of Awards

Made under Section 6.9. A rank is conferred by decree of the Provost under the Great Seal, and the manner of presentation does not affect validity (Section 6.9(1)).
1. The Legate: A Legate is a Warden, a Chaplain, or a Collegian holding a rank or an honour under Section 5.5 (including HonFHASRC and FHASRC (Em.)) in good standing, appointed by the Provost or, under Section 4.6, by the Secretary-General, by Letter of Delegation for a stated occasion, which may be as short as one day. Where no chartered branch exists in an area, the Provost may appoint any available Collegian there. A person who holds no rank or honour is not appointed Legate, whatever other office or orders that person holds. A Warden or Chaplain of another branch may be appointed Legate to present awards at a branch that person visits.
2. Rank of Presentation: A Legate who is a Warden or Chaplain acts as the arm of the Provost and may present any rank. Any other Legate may present only a rank equal to or lower than the Legate’s own (a holder of the Honorary or Emeritus Fellowship counting as a Fellow), save that an Associate may present an Associate only where the Letter of Delegation says so.
3. Dress: A Legate wears the regalia of the Legate’s own rank, in full as for a Solemn Occasion under Ordinance 11.5, and a Warden or Chaplain, including one of another branch, also wears the medallion of office. A Legate who holds neither office wears no medallion. A Legate is a high officer for the occasion for which appointed, and for that occasion only.
4. Record: The date, the place and the Legate are noted in the Register against the award.

Ordinance 11.2Regalia of the Fellow, the Honorary Fellow and the Fellow Emeritus

Every substantive Fellow (FHASRC), including the Founding Fellow, the Provost and the Provost Emeritus, wears the Cambridge MA gown and the yellow-gold satin hood lined in crimson-red satin, with the cowl edged in white faux ermine fur and the cape bound in crimson-red satin. The Honorary Fellow (HonFHASRC) and the Fellow Emeritus (FHASRC (Em.)) wear the same gown and hood, with one difference: the cape edge is plain, with no crimson binding. The Honorary Fellowship and the Emeritus Fellowship are honours under Section 5.5 of the Statutes. They stand outside the ladder of Associate, Licentiate and Fellow, and are neither below nor above the Fellow. The crimson binding of the cape is the mark of the substantive Fellowship. A Fellow who holds the substantive Fellowship keeps it for life, and keeps the bound hood, whether or not the Fellow is styled Emeritus. The Provost and the Provost Emeritus wear the bound hood because the Provost’s Fellowship under Section 5.5(4) of the Statutes recognises service as Provost as a substantive Fellowship, and the Founding Fellow wears it under Section 5.5(3). No other alteration of cut, colour, trim or fabric is permitted.

Ordinance 11.3Robemaker Guidelines & Heraldic Palette

1. Sable (Black Cloth): Represents cosmological mystery, intellectual discipline, and holy humility. Must be a plain matte black worsted wool, Russell cord, or quality synthetic academic cloth.
2. Gules (Crimson-Red Satin): Represents the Mystic Rose (Sapientia) and the living fire of the Holy Spirit. Linings must be executed in rich crimson-red satin. The fibre is not prescribed, so that polyester satin is permitted. The binding of a Fellow’s hood cape and the red epitoge are cut in the same crimson-red satin.
3. Or (Yellow-Gold Satin): Represents the Ascending Phoenix (Spiritum) and illuminated wisdom. The shell of all Fellow hoods must be cut from plain bright yellow-gold satin, a clear golden yellow and not bronze or brown. The binding of the cape of the Licentiate hood is cut from the same yellow-gold satin.
4. White Faux Ermine Fur: Denotes supreme collegiate dignity and ecclesiastical seniority on the hood of the Fellow, the Honorary Fellow and the Fellow Emeritus alike. Trim must be a dense, pure-white short-pile synthetic fur measuring two inches (50 mm) in visible width along the cowl edge of the hood. On an epitoge each band of ermine marks a degree of service under Ordinance 11.4.

Ordinance 11.5Occasions of Dress

1. Ordinary Assembly: A Warden, and a Chaplain where there is one, wears the medallion at every regular assembly, over ordinary dress whether casual or formal, even when no gown or hood is worn. This is a duty of the office, because the medallion is insignia of office and not regalia of rank. Full regalia is not required at a regular assembly. When the Warden decides that an assembly is a dressed assembly, the gown and hood are worn, and a Warden or Chaplain who is a Senior, or who holds an office named in Ordinance 11.4, also wears the epitoge. At a casual assembly a Senior wears the medallion only, like any other Warden or Chaplain.
2. Solemn Occasions: The gown and hood are worn at a convocation or presentation of diplomas, an installation or investiture of an officer wherever held, an anniversary of the Collegium, the Feast of Holy Sophia where regalia is worn, and any gathering at which a high officer is present. Those who hold an epitoge wear it with the gown, and those in office wear the medallion as well.
3. High Officer: A high officer is the Provost, the Acting Provost, the Coadjutor Provost, the Provost-Designate, the Secretary-General, a Pro-Chancellor, a Prelate of ACOA or of a partner body, and a Legate of the Provost on the occasion for which appointed.
4. Right, Not Duty: Except for the medallion of a Warden or Chaplain under item 1, the wearing of regalia is a right of the Collegian. No Collegian loses any rank, standing or right by not wearing it.
5. The Meaning of Regalia: Regalia is the oldest language of the learned and the faithful. The gown and hood descend from the habit and cowl of the cloister and the medieval schools, where the scholar and the monk put on the dress of their calling as a sign that they had given themselves to study, prayer and service. The medallion on its ribbon belongs to the long tradition of the badge of office, and the epitoge to the shoulder band of the doctor and the magistrate, carried as a sign of a burden borne for others. In the Collegium every part has its meaning under Ordinances 11.3 and 11.4: black for humility and the mystery to be studied, crimson for the living fire of the Holy Spirit, gold for illumined wisdom, and white ermine for dignity. In the science of sacrament an outward sign is given to turn the mind toward an inward reality, and the vestment does the same, so that to put it on is a small rite of passage which sets apart a time for the work of the Spirit. In the plain light of psychology, a fixed dress is a threshold: it helps the wearer to leave off ordinary haste and to take up the attention, patience and care that study and service ask, and it lets others see, and trust, one who has openly taken on a duty. The same black gown also levels, for beneath it no one is measured by wealth or fashion. Regalia is not for display or pride, and each Collegian is invited to wear it as a reminder of the vocation of study and service, and to put it on with a quiet thought or a short prayer.
6. A Guide for Vesting: It is both the honour and the duty of a Collegian who wears regalia to put it on with a quiet and attentive mind, as item 5 explains. The following guide is offered for that purpose. The words are a guide and not a form. Using them is not compulsory, and no Collegian loses any rank, standing or right, including the right to wear regalia, by saying other words or none. As each piece is put on, the Collegian may say, silently or aloud, in these or similar words:
(a) Beginning. “In the name of the Triune God, may I be of service to the Collegium and to humanity.”
(b) The gown. “As I put on this gown, may I humbly learn the path of wisdom.”
(c) The hood, the sign of the steps of study. “As I put on this hood, may I strive on the path of knowledge.”
(d) The epitoge, for those entitled to one. “As I put on this epitoge, may I bear the burden of the Collegium gladly.”
(e) The medallion, for those in office. “As I wear this medallion, may I keep faith with the Statutes.”
7. Dress Beneath the Regalia: The gown is worn over formal or business dress, of a man or of a woman, or over national or ceremonial dress of equal formality, and the dress beneath is neat and modest. A cleric of any minor or major order may instead wear beneath the gown the cassock or the choir dress of the cleric’s own tradition, and a bishop may wear the cassock or the episcopal choir dress of that tradition. The hood, the epitoge and the medallion are worn over clerical dress in the usual way. No one is refused a place in a procession for the cut or the colour of the dress beneath, so long as it is neat, modest and of a formality equal to the occasion.
8. Persons Without a Rank: An officer who holds no rank or honour, being a Provost-Designate or an Episcopal Confrater (neither of whom Section 4.8(6) of the Statutes requires to hold one), wears the plain black gown of the Cambridge BA pattern [b2], without a hood, in place of the regalia of a rank. The gown without a hood shows service within the scholar-ministry of the Collegium and does not show a rank. A Provost-Designate wears the epitoge over it, as Ordinance 11.4(7) provides, and an Episcopal Confrater may instead wear the choir dress that item 7 allows. A Civil Steward, and a Treasurer or an Examiner of Accounts who is a civil appointee, wear no gown or hood, and the Treasurer or Examiner wears the medallion only, as Ordinance 11.4(2) provides, unless the Provost confers an honour on the person under Section 4.8(6)(c) of the Statutes. A person who holds a rank or an honour wears the regalia of that rank or honour and not the gown of this item.
