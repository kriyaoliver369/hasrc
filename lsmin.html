Charism & Purpose

Liturgical Mastery & Altar Energetics

The Licentiate of Sacramental Ministry (L.S.Min) provides structured academic, contemplative, and rubrical formation for individuals preparing for clerical orders or seeking advanced mastery in independent sacramental liturgies[cite: 1]. Rooted in the convergent apostolic inheritance of Patriarch Herman Adrian Spruit and Archbishop Frank R. Bugge, the syllabus treats liturgy as both an objective conduit of divine grace and a living science of subtle energy[cite: 2].

Candidates investigate classical Catholic sacramental validity, historical liturgical evolution, the clairvoyant altar energetics codified by Charles Leadbeater, the homiletic proclamation of the Living Word, and the ministry of sacred music and sound[cite: 1, 2].

It grants the Collegium’s ecclesiastical licence to teach (licentia docendi) within a sacred discipline[cite: 2]. See the legal notice below.

Non-Automaticity of Holy Orders

Completion of the L.S.Min certifies rigorous academic, liturgical, and spiritual preparation, but does not per se confer, mandate, or guarantee ordination to the Diaconate, Priesthood, or Episcopate in the Australian Church of Antioch or any Independent Sacramental jurisdiction[cite: 2]. Holy Orders remain an independent act of episcopal discernment, character vetting, and canonical sponsorship under the candidate's ordaining prelate[cite: 2].

The program examines scholarship and practical liturgical competence; no rank is conferred as a certificate of personal spiritual attainment[cite: 2].

Canonical Pathways

Three Routes to Admission

Candidates enter the Licentiate ladder through one of three assessed routes under the foundational statutes[cite: 1, 2]. All routes are 100% free of tuition and assessment fees[cite: 2].

Route A

Full Curricular Track

Open to all qualified seekers and ordinands[cite: 1]. Candidates complete the six dedicated Subject Modules and an 8,000-word Capstone Treatise within a 12-month self-paced window[cite: 1]. Associates admitted under Route A complete Modules 4–6 and the Treatise[cite: 1].

Deliverable: 6 Essays + Capstone Treatise[cite: 1]
Route B

Ministerial & APEL Portfolio

Open to seasoned clergy serving in apostolic, autocephalous, or independent sacramental traditions (Permanent Deacons, Priests, or Bishops) holding at least 5 years of active or cumulative ministerial service with verified lineage documentation[cite: 1].

Deliverable: Portfolio + Capstone Treatise[cite: 1]
Route C

Graduate Degree Bridge

Open to holders of an earned Master’s degree (M.A., M.Sc., M.Div., M.Th.) from a recognised or independent seminary[cite: 1]. Grants advanced standing for up to 3 core modules (50% waiver), completing the remaining modules and Capstone Treatise[cite: 1].

Deliverable: Transcripts + 3 Modules / Treatise[cite: 1]
Curricular Architecture

The Six Subject Modules

Structured into a 3-module foundational sequence leading to the Associate rank (AHASRC / A.S.Min), followed by three upper-division licentiate modules and the culminating Capstone Treatise[cite: 1, 2].

Assessment Rigour

Examination Protocol & Academic Apparatus

Candidates compile their deliverables into a single Consolidated Examination Folio transmitted to the Chancery[cite: 1]. All research adheres strictly to the formal standards of the Collegium[cite: 1, 2].

The 8,000-Word Capstone Treatise

The culminating requirement is an original 8,000-word Liturgical & Ministerial Treatise structured in the customary theological five-chapter format[cite: 1]:

  • 1. Introduction & Research Question[cite: 1]
  • 2. Review of Theological Literature[cite: 1]
  • 3. Method, Liturgical Sources & Apparatus[cite: 1]
  • 4. Analysis & Ministerial Discussion[cite: 1]
  • 5. Conclusion & Pastoral Recommendations[cite: 1]

Must cite a minimum of twenty-five (25) primary, patristic, archival, or peer-reviewed sources referenced exclusively in APA 7th Edition style[cite: 1].

Evaluation & Collegiate Review

Every submission is assessed externally under the Dean of Studies and at least two collegiate assessors without conflict of interest[cite: 2]:

  • • Prior Approval: Candidates submit a 300-word topic proposal and provisional bibliography to the Dean of Studies before writing[cite: 1].
  • • Engagement with Charism: Work must critically engage at least one of the five foundational pillars of the Spruitian Charism[cite: 1].
  • • Fresh Work: Research must be newly written or substantial completion of unpublished work, checked for originality[cite: 1].
  • • Decision: Aimed within 90 days of complete folio submission[cite: 2].
