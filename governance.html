Ordo Collegii

How we are led

By people who serve. Every office here, from the Provost to your local Warden, exists to look after you.

Meet the household

You will see titles on this page: Provost, Dean, Custodian, Canon, Warden. They are real, and the people who hold them wear their gowns and medallions with gladness. But in this household a title is a way of describing what someone carries for others. It is not a rung to stand on above them. The Collegium takes its pattern from one sentence, and everything below is an attempt to live it.

“Whoever wants to be first must be last of all and servant of all.”

Mark 9:35

Spiritual parent and servant-minister

The Provost

The Collegium was founded in the person of its Founder, the Most Reverend Mar Elisha Thoma, who serves as Provost and Prior-General. We think of the Provost as a spiritual parent of the household first, and as an administrator second.

A good parent does not stand above the family, but among them, and goes first in the work.

Keeps the vision and the prayer

The Provost watches over what the Collegium is for: study freely offered, prayer kept in common, and learning that serves the Church and the seeker alike.

Puts a name to your work

Every diploma is signed by the Provost and sealed with the Great Seal. Your award is received from the hand of the one who leads us, not from an office.

Calls co-servants and listens to them

The Provost invites Fellows, Canons and officers to share the work, and leans on their counsel. No one is meant to carry a household alone.

Serves as a ministry

The Collegium is not-for-profit. Leadership here is a ministry of service, held in trust for the Collegium’s vision and mission, and what students receive is freely offered.

Co-servants in one household

The offices, and who they serve

The Provost does not work alone. Each office carries one part of the Collegium’s care, in unity with the others. Tap any office to see what it does for you.

Who walk with your study

teachers, readers and keepers of the library

i.The Dean of StudiesYour guide through the programmes.+

The Dean welcomes your petition, reads your work alongside other assessors, credits what you have already learned elsewhere, and agrees your research topic with you before you begin writing.

If you need more time, you write to the Dean.

ii.The Editor-in-ChiefGives your research a place to be heard.+

The Editor-in-Chief looks after the Journal of Inner Spirituality and its monographs, and invites scholars to read each submission fairly, so your article is judged by people who know the subject.

iii.The Keeper of the ArchivesGuards the memory we all inherit.+

The Keeper tends the digital repositories and primary sources of the Spruit-Bugge heritage, so students and researchers have a living library to draw on.

iv.The Academic SenateEvery substantive Fellow has a seat.+

Fellows are the scholarly heart of the Collegium. They review articles, read petitions in small panels of three, sponsor candidates and advise on research. A Fellow gives their time to lift the next student.

Who keep the household

quiet work that makes the rest possible

i.The Secretary-GeneralThe Provost’s helping hands, day to day.+

Answers letters, keeps routine matters moving and may sign diplomas in the Provost’s name when asked, so that no student waits longer than they should.

ii.The Custodian of the RegisterKeeps faith with every award.+

The Custodian keeps the Public Register of Awards, the open record that lets anyone confirm what you earned, and signs first on each diploma as the Chancery’s pass.

iii.The Treasurer & Examiner of AccountsGood stewards of a freely given gift.+

The Treasurer looks after the Collegium’s funds, and a separate Examiner reviews the accounts every year, so that what is given in trust is handled with care.

iv.The Elder CouncilWise friends at the Provost’s side.+

A small circle of trusted elders who lend the Provost counsel, so that decisions are weighed in company.

Who pray and counsel

honour given in friendship, with no power attached

i.The CanonsBishop, priest, deacon or layperson.+

Canons give counsel and keep the common prayer of the Collegium. Ordination is not required; a layperson is styled Lay Canon. Theirs is an office of prayer and wisdom, not of command.

ii.The Pro-ChancellorsPrelates who bless our milestones.+

Prelates of high standing whom the Provost invites. They may countersign diplomas, bless convocations and graduations, carry the Collegium’s goodwill to sister bodies and give counsel when asked.

iii.The Episcopal ConfraterA spiritual parent and friend of the Collegium.+

An honorary mandate held by the Presiding Archbishop of the ACOA, who stands with us in counsel and prayer. It carries no administrative power, only friendship and blessing.

iv.LegatesThe Provost’s hand at your graduation.+

For a single occasion, the Provost may send a Legate to present awards in person, so your milestone can be celebrated close to home.

The honour of serving

An office is a gift given, not a prize won

Those who hold office here count it an honour, and the honour is the service itself. Three things hold true of every office on this page.

Freely given

Offices are voluntary and unpaid. Officers receive, at most, the cost of their documented expenses. No one is here for a salary, and no title can be bought.

Held for the mission

Each office is held in trust for the Collegium’s vision and mission: sacred learning freely offered, prayer kept in common, and the Spruitian charism handed on faithfully. It is never used for personal ambition.

Lent, not owned

The medallion goes back when the office ends. Collegians serve in the spirit of sacred scholarship, Christian stewardship and mutual intellectual advancement, and the work always outlasts the one doing it.

Closest to you

Wardens and Chaplains

For most members, the Collegium has a face, and it is a local one. Each Chapter and Oratory is led by people its own members trust.

The Warden

Presides over a branch, welcomes newcomers and keeps the local gathering warm and orderly. A Warden wears the medallion of office at every regular assembly, over ordinary dress, so that everyone knows whom to ask.

The Chaplain

An Oratory may have a Chaplain, an ordained priest or prelate who leads its prayer and cares for souls. The Chaplain is a pastor to the members, close enough to know their names.

Chosen by you

A branch’s members choose their own Warden and Chaplain. The Chancery equips them with guidance, a medallion, study materials and visiting speakers, and does not impose a leader on a community.

After five years of faithful service, a Warden or Chaplain is recognised as Senior Warden or Senior Chaplain, an honour for life. It is thanks, not rank.

In unity

Many gifts, one body

The Provost, the officers, the Fellows, the Canons and the Wardens are not a ladder. They are one household, each giving what they have been given. Students are not at the bottom of it. You are the reason it exists.

Free to learn

Study is offered as a ministry. The officers who guide your study give their time without stipend.

Carefully assessed

Real readers weigh your work, and every award is entered in the Public Register for anyone to verify.

Held in trust

The Collegium is not-for-profit. Whatever it holds is held for the work of sacred learning, never for private gain.

Academic dress

Gowns, medallions & epitoges

The Collegium follows the British collegiate tradition. Dress shows three different things, and the last of them is the one we love best.

Your award

The gown & hood

Associates and Licentiates wear the black Cambridge BA gown with a crimson-lined hood; the Licentiate’s hood carries a 1-inch gold binding. Fellows wear the Cambridge MA gown with a gold doctoral hood trimmed with white faux ermine.

Your office

The medallion

A medallion on a ribbon belongs to the office, not the person: issued when an office is confirmed, returned when it ends. It is a reminder that the work is lent to us and is never ours to keep.

Your service

The epitoge

A pleated panel of cloth over the left shoulder, the sign of service: carrying the Collegium’s burdens for one another as a servant of all (cf. Mark 9:35; John 13). After five full years in good standing, an officer may keep it for life.

Medallion ribbons

  • The Provost
    Red and gold · the Great Seal
  • Provost-Designate, Secretary-General, Acting and Coadjutor Provost
    Red · the Collegium’s arms
  • Pro-Chancellor
    Black and gold · the Collegium’s arms
  • Custodian, Dean of Studies, Treasurer, Keeper of the Archives, Editor-in-Chief, Wardens and Chaplains
    Black · the Collegium’s arms

Epitoges

  • Provost and Provost Emeritus
    Red, three bands
  • Secretary-General, Pro-Chancellor and those who serve nearest the Provost
    Black, three bands
  • Custodian, Dean of Studies, Keeper of the Archives, Editor-in-Chief
    Black, two bands
  • Treasurer and Examiner of Accounts, Senior Warden, Senior Chaplain
    Black, one band

A Senior Warden or Senior Chaplain keeps the one-band epitoge for life. An officer who leaves before five years stops wearing it but keeps the cloth they made.
