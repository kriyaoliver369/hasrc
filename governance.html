Ordo Collegii

How we are led

One steward to hold the vision, many to counsel, and an independent voice to keep everyone honest.

See the order

Every learned society has someone who decides, people who advise, and a way of keeping everyone honest. Below is how HASRC arranges them. Tap any role to read more.

The steward

The Provost & Founder

The Collegium was founded in the person of its Founder, the Most Reverend Mar Elisha Thoma, who serves as Provost and Prior-General for life. The Provost holds the final say on matters such as changing the Statutes, conferring awards and caring for the Collegium’s property, and keeps the Great Seal.

One clear voice, so the Collegium’s purpose is never pulled apart by factions.

Administers

who keep the work moving

i. The Secretary-GeneralKeeps the Collegium running day to day, in the Provost’s name. +

The Provost may appoint a Secretary-General for routine administration and whatever the Provost lists in a written Schedule of Delegations, which can include signing diplomas in the Provost’s name.

The Secretary-General acts for the Provost, never in place of the Provost, and does not become Provost by holding the post.

ii. The Custodian of the RegisterGuards the registers and the sealed decree of succession. +

The Custodian keeps the Collegium’s registers and holds the Provost’s sealed decree naming a successor, unopened, until it is needed. The Custodian also appoints the arbitrators, which keeps that choice at arm’s length from the Provost.

Advises

who lend counsel

i. The Academic SenateEvery substantive Fellow has a seat. +

The Senate reviews articles for the Journal, reads petitions through Review Panels of three, sponsors candidates and advises on research and archives.

Its voice is consultative: it examines, recommends and advises. Awards are conferred only as the Statutes provide.

ii. The Elder CouncilA small council the Provost may form, of three, five or seven. +

While the Provost is reachable it simply advises, and the Provost is free to follow or not.

It acts for the Provost in only two cases: the Provost cannot be reached for more than 45 days, or the Provost asks it to act by written Letter. That keeps the Collegium steady if the Provost is ever away.

Checks

who keep it honest

i. The Council of ArbitrationEnsures no one judges their own case. +

Three permanent members, each appointed by the Custodian for a renewable five-year term. Their purpose is that no weighty question, such as a petition to remove a Provost, is decided by someone with a stake in it.

For certain grave petitions four more are drawn by lot, so seven sit. All are Collegians of good standing with at least five years’ standing.

ii. The Founder’s reserve authorityA personal safeguard, used only to protect the Collegium. +

The Founder holds a personal, non-transferable authority, used only if the Provost’s act or omission would endanger the Collegium’s existence or continuity. It sits in reserve while the Founder is Provost.

iii. Declaration of VacancyA last resort, never for mere disagreement. +

If a Provost became a grave danger to the Collegium, the office can be declared vacant through a defined process involving the Council of Arbitration. Disagreement, dissent or private belief is never a ground.

Looking ahead

When a Provost’s service ends

The Collegium is built to outlast any one person. The plan is written and sealed in advance, so there is never a scramble.

  1. I

    A sealed decree

    The Provost names a successor, and alternates in order, in a decree sealed with the Great Seal.

  2. II

    Kept unopened

    The Custodian holds it. No one is asked to accept before it is opened.

  3. III

    Opened when needed

    If the Provost is incapacitated or passes, the Custodian opens it without delay and gives public notice.

  4. IV

    Seven days to accept

    The person named may accept in writing and becomes the Provost-Designate. The Collegium carries on meanwhile.

  5. V

    Installation

    The Presiding Archbishop of the ACOA, as Episcopal Confrater, sees the Provost-Designate trained, ordained and consecrated as needed, then installed.

Until the installation, the Custodian is caretaker and the Provost-Designate handles routine matters, so students and Fellows notice no gap.

Honorary & ceremonial

Those who lend counsel and dignity

These offices honour and support. They do not hold the decision.

Ceremonial

Pro-Chancellors

Prelates of high standing whom the Provost invites for a three-year term. They may countersign diplomas, bless convocations and graduations, represent the Collegium’s goodwill to sister bodies and give counsel when asked.

Counsel & prayer

Canons

Collegians honoured to give counsel and keep the common prayer of the Collegium. Bishop, priest, deacon or layperson may serve (a layperson is styled Lay Canon). A role of counsel, not of governance.

An honour

The Episcopal Confrater

An honorary mandate held by the Presiding Archbishop of the ACOA: a father in God and friend of the Collegium, serving at the time of succession through counsel, prayer and training. It carries no administrative power.

The Collegium is not-for-profit, and its officers serve without stipend. Should it ever close, its assets pass to a kindred jurisdiction, library or trust, never to individuals.

Academic dress

Gowns, medallions & epitoges

The Collegium follows the British collegiate tradition. Dress shows three different things, and it helps to keep them apart.

Your award

The gown & hood

Associates and Licentiates wear the black Cambridge BA gown with a crimson-lined hood; the Licentiate’s hood carries a 1-inch gold binding. Fellows wear the Cambridge MA gown with a gold doctoral hood trimmed with white faux ermine.

Your office

The medallion

A medallion on a ribbon belongs to the office, not the person: issued when an office is confirmed, returned when it ends. A Warden wears it over ordinary dress at every regular assembly; full gown and hood are for solemn occasions.

Your service

The epitoge

A pleated panel of cloth over the left shoulder, the sign of service: carrying the Collegium’s burdens for one another as a servant of all. After five full years in good standing, an officer may keep it for life.

Medallion ribbons

  • The Provost
    Red and gold · the Great Seal
  • Provost-Designate, Secretary-General, Acting and Coadjutor Provost
    Red · the Collegium’s arms
  • Pro-Chancellor
    Black and gold · the Collegium’s arms
  • Custodian, arbitrators, Dean of Studies, Treasurer, Keeper of the Archives, Editor-in-Chief, Wardens and Chaplains
    Black · the Collegium’s arms

Epitoges

  • Provost and Provost Emeritus
    Red, three bands
  • Provost-Designate, Secretary-General, Pro-Chancellor, Acting and Coadjutor Provost
    Black, three bands
  • Custodian, Dean of Studies, Keeper of the Archives, Editor-in-Chief, arbitrators
    Black, two bands
  • Treasurer and Examiner of Accounts, Senior Warden, Senior Chaplain
    Black, one band

A Senior Warden or Senior Chaplain keeps the one-band epitoge for life. An officer who leaves before five years stops wearing it but keeps the cloth they made.
